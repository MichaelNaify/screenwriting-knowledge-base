## DIALOGUE

**Universal Definition**: The spoken words of characters that reveal personality, advance plot, create conflict, convey theme, and establish relationships, ideally operating through subtext rather than direct statement.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Seger (Making Great), Apatow (Sick in Head), Finn (Sitcom), Holloway (Joke Writing)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Subtext primary; dialogue as action
- Seger: Character voice differentiation; problem-solving
- Apatow: Authentic voice from personal truth
- Holloway: Comedy dialogue mechanics
- Finn: Television-specific dialogue craft

**Tags**: #dialogue #dialogue_foundation #subtext #character_voice #mckee #seger #apatow #finn #holloway

## DRAMATIC NEED

**Universal Definition** (Field): What the protagonist wants to achieve - the goal that drives the story forward and organizes the protagonist's actions throughout the screenplay.

**Source Coverage**: Field (Screenplay), McKee (Story - as "desire"), Truby (Anatomy - as "desire"), Seger (Making Great)

**Key Variations**:
- Field: "Dramatic need" - specific, achievable goal
- McKee: "Object of desire" - conscious want
- Truby: "Desire" - drives character through story
- Seger: Goal that creates spine of story

**Tags**: #character #structure #dramatic_need #desire #goal #field #mckee #truby

---

## ESCALATION

**Universal Definition**: The progressive intensification of conflict, stakes, comedy, or action as the story develops, creating mounting tension and audience engagement.

**Source Coverage**: Vorhaus (Comic Toolbox), Blake (Comedy Movie), Holloway (Joke Writing), McKee (Story - as "progressive complication"), Johnstone (Impro - as "heightening")

**Key Variations**:
- Vorhaus: Comic escalation - systematic building
- Blake: Comedy intensity increase
- Holloway: Joke escalation through toppers
- McKee: Progressive complication in drama
- Johnstone: Heightening through acceptance

**Tags**: #structure #comedy #escalation #building #progressive_complication #vorhaus #blake #mckee

---

## EXPOSITION

**Universal Definition**: Information about characters, relationships, or events that occurred before the story begins, or off-screen during the story, that the audience needs to understand the narrative.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat - "Pope in the Pool"), Finn (Sitcom)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Hide exposition in conflict; make dramatic
- Field: Provide only necessary exposition
- Seger: Spread throughout; avoid dumps
- Snyder: "Pope in the Pool" - exposition with action
- Finn: Exposition challenges in sitcom

**Tags**: #dialogue #structure #exposition #information #mckee #field #seger #snyder

## GAME OF THE SCENE

**Universal Definition** (Close): The comedic pattern or unusual behavior discovered in an improv scene that becomes the focus for heightening and exploration.

**Source Coverage**: Close (Truth in Comedy), Besser (Manual), Finn (Sitcom - applied to scripted)

**Key Variations**:
- Close: Find game, heighten game, explore game
- Besser: UCB game-finding methodology
- Finn: Game concept applied to sitcom writing

**Tags**: #comedy_improv #improvisation #game #pattern #close #besser #finn

## GENRE

**Universal Definition**: Categories of storytelling defined by shared conventions, audience expectations, and storytelling patterns (e.g., thriller, romance, horror, comedy).

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Snyder (Save the Cat), Blake (Comedy Movie), Truby (Anatomy), Owen (Adaptation)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Genre as worldview; deep genre understanding
- Snyder: 10 genre types with specific patterns
- Blake: Comedy genre subdivisions
- Truby: Genre as audience contract
- Owen: Genre considerations in adaptation

**Tags**: #genre #structure #worldview #convention #mckee #snyder #blake #truby

## HEIGHTENING

**Universal Definition**: The improv technique of taking an established pattern or behavior and intensifying it, making it bigger, more specific, or more absurd while remaining true to the pattern.

**Source Coverage**: Johnstone (Impro), Close (Truth in Comedy), Besser (Manual)

**Key Variations**:
- Johnstone: Through acceptance and status
- Close: Finding and heightening game
- Besser: Systematic heightening technique

**Tags**: #comedy_improv #improvisation #heightening #escalation #johnstone #close #besser

---

## INCITING INCIDENT

**Universal Definition**: The event that disrupts the protagonist's life, creates the central dramatic question, and launches the story into motion.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Truby (Anatomy), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat - "Catalyst"), Owen (Adaptation)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Radically upsets balance of forces
- Field: Occurs around page 10-15
- Truby: "Opportunity" - first of 22 building blocks
- Snyder: "Catalyst" - happens around page 12
- Seger: Establishes central conflict

**Tags**: #structure #inciting_incident #catalyst #opportunity #mckee #field #truby #snyder

---

## JOKE STRUCTURE

**Universal Definition**: The technical construction of a joke, typically consisting of setup (establishes premise/context), punchline (delivers unexpected twist), and optional tag (additional humor layered on punchline).

**Source Coverage**: Holloway (Serious Guide), Rosenfield (Stand-Up), Vorhaus (Comic Toolbox), Blake (Comedy Movie)

**Key Variations**:
- Holloway: Most comprehensive joke mechanics
- Rosenfield: Stand-up specific applications
- Vorhaus: Jokes within comedy structure
- Blake: Joke structure in screenplay context

**Tags**: #comedy_jokes #joke_writing #setup #punchline #tag #holloway #rosenfield #vorhaus

---

## MIDPOINT

**Universal Definition**: The structural beat at the center of the screenplay (typically page 60 in 120-page script) that often features a false victory or defeat and shifts the story's direction.

**Source Coverage**: Field (Screenplay), Snyder (Save the Cat), Seger (Making Great)

**Key Variations**:
- Field: Center of Act Two; raises stakes
- Snyder: "Midpoint" - false victory/defeat
- Seger: Midpoint problem-solving

**Tags**: #structure #midpoint #turning_point #field #snyder #seger

---

## MORAL PREMISE

**Universal Definition** (Truby): The moral or philosophical argument the story makes, expressed through the protagonist's character change from moral weakness to moral strength (or tragedy: weakness to greater weakness).

**Source Coverage**: Truby (Anatomy), McKee (Story - as "controlling idea"), Seger (Making Great - as "theme")

**Key Variations**:
- Truby: Foundation of story design
- McKee: Similar concept as "controlling idea"
- Seger: Related to theme expression

**Tags**: #theme #moral_premise #controlling_idea #truby #mckee #theme_theory
