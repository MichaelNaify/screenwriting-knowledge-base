#### **2.3 Phase 2: Genre-Specific Structure Building (Part 4)**

**Thriller Decision Tree** (Part 4, Section 3.3):

**Question 1:** Is your thriller plot-driven or character-driven?
- **Analysis:** Gaslighting theme suggests character focus BUT tech startup setting suggests plot complexity
- **Decision:** HYBRID - character psychology drives plot mechanism

**Question 2:** What is the central mystery or question?
- **Articulation:** "Is the protagonist losing her mind or is someone deliberately manipulating her reality?"
- **Stakes:** Career, sanity, safety

**Question 3:** Information revelation pattern - gradual or sudden?

### CASE STUDY 3: Adaptation Challenges
**"Translating Novel to Screenplay Using Complete System"**

#### **3.1 Project Context**
- **Writer:** Experienced screenwriter, hired for adaptation
- **Source Material:** 400-page literary novel, non-linear structure, internal narration heavy
- **Project Goal:** 110-page screenplay maintaining emotional essence
- **Timeline:** 16 weeks (treatment + 2 drafts)

#### **3.2 Phase 1: Diagnostic Assessment of Source (Part 2)**

**Adaptation-Specific Diagnostic** (Part 2, Section 5):

**Source Material Analysis:**
- Structure: Non-linear (5 timelines interwoven)
- POV: First-person, deeply internal
- Narrative engine: Character reflection > external plot
- Page count: 400 pages → target 110 screenplay
- Compression ratio: 3.6:1

**Key Challenges Identified:**
1. Converting internal monologue to external action
2. Linearizing timeline while preserving emotional logic
3. Maintaining three-dimensional supporting cast with reduced screen time
4. Preserving literary style without voice-over dependence

**System Routes To:**
- Part 4, Section 4: "Adaptation Strategy Decision Tree"
- Part 7, Section 6: "Adaptation-Specific Problem Solving"
- Part 5, Section 6: "Adaptation Technique Library"

#### **3.3 Phase 2: Strategic Adaptation Decisions (Part 4)**

**Adaptation Strategy Tree** (Part 4, Section 4):

**Question 1: Structural Approach**
- **Options:**
  - A: Preserve non-linear structure (challenging but distinctive)
  - B: Full linearization (accessible but loses unique quality)
  - C: Partial linearization (two timelines maximum)
  
**Decision:** Option C - Present day + one flashback timeline
- **Rationale:** Maintains narrative sophistication while ensuring clarity
- **Technical approach:** Present story (80%) with flashback spine (20%) at key emotional moments

**Question 2: POV Translation**
- **Options:**
  - A: Voice-over narration (preserves author's voice)
  - B: Visual storytelling only (cinematic but loses interiority)
  - C: Hybrid (strategic voice-over at key moments)
  
**Decision:** Option B with technique modifications
- **Rationale:** Avoid voice-over trap, use "Ghost as Present Action" technique to externalize past

**Question 3: Character Compression**
- Novel has 12 significant characters
- Screenplay can support 6-8 meaningfully
- **Decision:** Composite technique - merge 4 minor characters into 2 substantial ones

#### **3.4 Phase 3: Technique Application for Adaptation (Part 5)**

**Adaptation-Specific Techniques:**

**For Structure Translation:**
- **Technique #155:** "Emotional Beats as Structure" (Owen/Hutcheon) - preserve feeling over events
- **Technique #158:** "Temporal Anchoring" - establish clear timeline markers
- **Technique #160:** "Flashback as Present Pressure" (Truby) - past only appears when present demands it

**For Interiority Externalization:**
- **Technique #162:** "Subtext Through Behavior" (Seger) - thought becomes action
- **Technique #165:** "Object as Memory" - physical items trigger emotional states
- **Technique #168:** "Relationship as Mirror" - internal conflict becomes interpersonal

**For Compression:**
- **Technique #170:** "Character Fusion Protocol" - combining without losing dimension
- **Technique #172:** "Scene Function Stacking" - one scene serves multiple narrative purposes
- **Technique #175:** "Exposition Through Conflict" (McKee) - information emerges from dramatic need

#### **3.5 Phase 4: Workflow - Treatment Development (Part 3)**

**Week 1-2: Structural Architecture** (Part 3, Section 4.1):

**Timeline Mapping:**
- Present timeline: Character dealing with mother's illness
- Flashback timeline: Character's childhood with same parent, establishing relationship pattern
- Integration points: 6 major flashbacks at emotional pressure points
  - Act 1: 1 flashback (establishing relationship)
  - Act 2A: 2 flashbacks (complicating history)
  - Act 2B: 2 flashbacks (revelation of pattern)
  - Act 3: 1 flashback (resolution of past = resolution of present)

**Emotional Beat Sheet:**
Using Technique #155, map novel's emotional journey:
- Novel's emotional arc: Denial → Anger → Bargaining → Depression → Acceptance (Kübler-Ross applied to relationship)
- Screenplay structure: Map these 5 emotional states to 5 sequences
- Each flashback deepens understanding of current emotional state

**Week 3-4: Character Consolidation:**

**Character Fusion Process** (Technique #170):
- Novel characters: Sister, Best Friend, Therapist, Neighbor (4 separate voices)
- Screenplay composite: Sister (incorporates best friend support) + Nurse (incorporates therapist wisdom + neighbor observation)
- Test: Does composite feel authentic or mechanical?
- Validation: Each combined character serves minimum 3 distinct functions

**Week 5-6: Scene-by-Scene Treatment:**

**Scene Function Stacking** (Technique #172):
Example scene in present timeline:
- Function 1: Protagonist argues with sister about mother's care (plot/conflict)
- Function 2: Argument reveals protagonist's control issues (character revelation)
- Function 3: Mother's reaction shows pattern from past (theme)
- Function 4: Medical crisis emerges from stress (escalation)
- **Result:** One 3-page scene accomplishes work of 4 separate novel chapters

#### **3.6 Phase 5: Problem-Solving - Voice-Over Temptation (Part 7)**

**Obstacle:** First draft relies too heavily on voice-over for internal thoughts

**Problem-Solving Protocol** (Part 7, Section 6.2):

**Diagnosis:**
- 15 scenes contain voice-over narration
- Average: 2-3 lines per scene
- Total: ~3 pages of voice-over in 110-page script

**Decision Tree:** When is voice-over justified?
- Question: Does it convey information impossible to show?
- Analysis: 80% could be converted to visual storytelling
- Question: Does it create unique narrative style?
- Analysis: Not distinctive enough to justify breaks in visual flow

**Solution Application:**
- **Technique #162:** Convert thought to behavior
  - Example: "I couldn't face her" (voice-over) → Character literally turns away, leaves room (action)
- **Technique #165:** Convert thought to object interaction
  - Example: "Memories flooded back" (voice-over) → Character finds old photo album, physical response (visual)
- **Technique #168:** Convert thought to dialogue/subtext
  - Example: "I never understood her" (voice-over) → Argument with sister reveals incomprehension (dramatic)

**Result:** Voice-over reduced from 15 scenes to 2 (opening and closing bookends only)
