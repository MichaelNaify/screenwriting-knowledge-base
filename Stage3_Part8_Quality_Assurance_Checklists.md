## 1. Quality Assurance Philosophy {#qa-philosophy}

### Core Principles

**Quality Assurance is Not Policing**  
QA systems function as creative support infrastructure, not creative gatekeeping. Every checklist serves to:
- **Illuminate** problems before they become crises
- **Validate** what's working well
- **Calibrate** writer intuition with measurable standards
- **Accelerate** development by preventing common errors

**Quality as Systematic Observation**  
From Chitlik's seven-pass system and Seger's revision methodology: quality emerges through *systematic observation* at different scales (macro → micro → macro) rather than single-pass judgment.

**Three Quality Dimensions**

1. **Technical Quality**: Structure, format, mechanics
2. **Craft Quality**: Character, dialogue, scene construction
3. **Artistic Quality**: Theme, emotional truth, originality

All three must meet minimum thresholds for professional viability.

### Quality Assurance Hierarchy

**Level 1: Functional Completion**  
Does the script *work* at basic levels?
- Story comprehensible
- Characters distinguishable
- Structure visible
- Format professional

**Level 2: Craft Excellence**  
Does the script demonstrate *mastery* of craft fundamentals?
- Structure optimal for story needs
- Characters compelling and consistent
- Dialogue efficient and characterful
- Scenes emotionally dynamic

**Level 3: Artistic Achievement**  
Does the script achieve *transcendent* storytelling?
- Theme profound and earned
- Emotional truth resonant
- Voice distinctive
- Cultural/genre contribution meaningful

**Target Thresholds by Development Stage**

| Stage | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| First Draft | 75% | 50% | 25% |
| Second Draft | 90% | 75% | 50% |
| Polish Draft | 95% | 90% | 75% |
| Professional Draft | 98% | 95% | 85% |

### Quality Metrics Integration

**Quantitative Measures** (from Chitlik, McKee, Truby systems):
- **E(t)**: Scene energy delta (≠ 0 for ≥95% of scenes)
- **Sσ**: Structural variance (≤5% from paradigm)
- **Θ**: Theme alignment score (≥0.85 at lock)
- **Dρ**: Dialogue efficiency ratio (≈0.6 dialogue/action)
- **κ**: Iteration cadence (≥7 focused passes)

**Qualitative Markers** (from Seger, McKee, Field):
- Emotional authenticity
- Thematic clarity
- Character consistency
- Tonal coherence
- Genre satisfaction

**Integration Point:**  
QA checklists translate between quantitative metrics and qualitative judgment, providing *concrete markers* for abstract standards.

## 2. Draft-Level Quality Gates {#draft-gates}

### Gate 1: First Draft Completion

**Minimum Viability Checklist**

**Structural Completeness (Snyder, Field, McKee)**
- [ ] **Beginning** clearly establishes world, character, ordinary life
- [ ] **Inciting incident** disrupts status quo (by page 10-15)
- [ ] **First act turn** commits protagonist to journey (by page 25-30)
- [ ] **Midpoint** shifts story direction or reveals information
- [ ] **Second act turn** darkest moment/all seems lost (by page 75-90)
- [ ] **Climax** resolves central dramatic question
- [ ] **Resolution** shows new equilibrium

**Character Foundation (Truby, Seger, McKee)**
- [ ] Protagonist has clear **conscious goal** (what they want)
- [ ] Protagonist has clear **unconscious need** (what they need)
- [ ] Protagonist demonstrates **moral weakness** requiring transformation
- [ ] Protagonist makes **active choices** driving story forward
- [ ] Antagonist provides **genuine opposition** with own valid goals
- [ ] **Character arc trajectory** visible from beginning to end

**Scene-Level Functionality (McKee, Field)**
- [ ] Every scene has identifiable **purpose** (setup/development/payoff/pivot)
- [ ] ≥85% of scenes demonstrate **value change** (emotional movement)
- [ ] Scene sequence demonstrates **causal logic** (A leads to B leads to C)
- [ ] **Pacing variation** present (fast/slow rhythm alternation)

**Dialogue Baseline (McKee, Vorhaus, Blake)**
- [ ] Characters have **distinguishable voices** (vocabulary/rhythm differences)
- [ ] Dialogue serves **dual function** (information + characterization)
- [ ] **Exposition** delivered obliquely (not on-the-nose)
- [ ] ≥50% of dialogue demonstrates **subtext** (conceals true intention)

**Format and Mechanics**
- [ ] Proper screenplay format throughout
- [ ] Consistent character name spelling
- [ ] Scene headings formatted correctly
- [ ] Page count within genre range (90-120 for features)

**First Draft Quality Score Target: 75/100**  
*Purpose: Establish complete story skeleton before detailed revision*

### Gate 2: Second Draft Enhancement

**Structural Optimization (Field, Snyder, Truby)**

**Act Balance Verification**
- [ ] Act I = 25-30% of total runtime/pages
- [ ] Act II = 50-55% of total runtime/pages  
- [ ] Act III = 20-25% of total runtime/pages
- [ ] **Variance** from paradigm ≤5% OR justified by genre/story needs

**Turning Point Integrity (Field, McKee, Snyder)**
- [ ] **Inciting Incident** clearly disrupts ordinary world
- [ ] **Act I Turn** commits protagonist to action (no turning back)
- [ ] **Midpoint** significantly shifts story direction/information
- [ ] **Act II Turn** creates maximum pressure/stakes
- [ ] **Climax** delivers satisfying resolution of dramatic question
- [ ] Each turning point demonstrates **value reversal** (+ to -, - to +)

**Setup/Payoff Audit (Field, Snyder, Seger)**
- [ ] Every **major setup** (character trait, object, relationship) pays off
- [ ] Every **payoff** has appropriate setup (no deus ex machina)
- [ ] Setup/payoff ratio ≥0.9 (90% completion)
- [ ] **Planting** occurs early enough for organic discovery
- [ ] **Payoff** delivers emotional satisfaction

**Character Depth (Truby, Seger, McKee)**

**Protagonist Development**
- [ ] **Moral weakness** clearly established in Act I
- [ ] **Ghost** (formative wound) implied or stated
- [ ] **Need vs Want** conflict generates internal pressure
- [ ] Character makes ≥3 **significant decisions** driving plot
- [ ] **Transformation moment** clearly dramatized
- [ ] Final character state demonstrates **change** or **steadfastness** (earned)

**Character Consistency Vector (Seger)**
- [ ] Consistency score ≥0.75 across all scenes
- [ ] Behavioral variations **motivated** by circumstances
- [ ] Speech patterns remain **distinctive** throughout
- [ ] Value system remains **coherent** (or transformation earned)

**Supporting Character Functionality**
- [ ] Each significant character serves **plot function** (ally/mentor/tempter)
- [ ] Each significant character serves **thematic function** (perspective on theme)
- [ ] **B-story relationships** provide contrast/illumination to A-story
- [ ] Character web demonstrates **interconnection** (not isolated bubbles)

**Dialogue Refinement (McKee, Vorhaus, Seger)**

**Efficiency Test**
- [ ] Could cut 25% of dialogue without losing essential meaning
- [ ] **Compression ratio** ≤0.33 (words per scene length)
- [ ] **Redundancy index** ≤10% (repeated information)
- [ ] **Exposition lines** ≤15% of total dialogue

**Subtext Density**
- [ ] Conflict scenes demonstrate ≥60% subtext
- [ ] Characters **speak around** what they truly mean
- [ ] **Gap** between text and subtext creates tension
- [ ] Audience can **infer** emotional truth beneath surface

**Scene-Level Quality (McKee, Chitlik, Field)**

**Scene Energy Audit**
- [ ] **ΔValue ≠ 0** for ≥90% of scenes (emotional movement occurs)
- [ ] **ΔEmotion ≥0.5** (sufficient emotional shift per scene)
- [ ] Scene purpose clearly identifiable: setup/development/payoff/pivot
- [ ] **Scene length** ≤3 pages average (exceptions justified)

**Second Draft Quality Score Target: 85/100**  
*Purpose: Optimize core story systems before final polish*

### Gate 3: Polish Draft Refinement

**Micro-Level Excellence (Seger, Chitlik, McKee)**

**Action Line Efficiency**
- [ ] Action lines ≤3-4 lines per paragraph
- [ ] **Visual specificity** present (shows rather than tells)
- [ ] **Active verbs** predominate over passive constructions
- [ ] **Economy** achieved (no redundant descriptions)

**Dialogue Polish**
- [ ] Each line serves **multiple functions** (character + plot + theme)
- [ ] **Rhythm variation** between characters (short/long patterns)
- [ ] **Interruptions** and **overlaps** create naturalism (where appropriate)
- [ ] **Silence** used strategically (reaction beats, emotional processing)

**Visual Storytelling Enhancement**
- [ ] ≥60% of story told through **visual action** vs dialogue
- [ ] **Image systems** (recurring visual motifs) present
- [ ] **Symbolic objects** earn thematic meaning through repetition
- [ ] Scene transitions demonstrate **visual logic**

**Thematic Clarity (Truby, Seger, McKee)**

**Theme Expression Audit**
- [ ] **Controlling idea** (theme) expressible in single sentence
- [ ] ≥3 characters embody **different perspectives** on theme
- [ ] **Protagonist's arc** proves/disproves thematic assertion
- [ ] Theme emerges **organically** from action (not stated directly)
- [ ] **Θ score** (theme alignment) ≥0.85 across all scenes

**Tonal Consistency (Seger, Blake, Vorhaus)**
- [ ] **Genre promises** fulfilled (thriller thrills, comedy laughs, etc.)
- [ ] **Tonal register** consistent throughout (shifts justified)
- [ ] **Emotional range** appropriate to genre/story
- [ ] **Voice** distinctive and maintained

**Polish Draft Quality Score Target: 92/100**  
*Purpose: Achieve professional presentation standards*

## 3. Element-Specific Validation Checklists {#element-checklists}

### Structure Checklist (Field, Snyder, McKee, Truby)

**Macro-Structure Verification**

**Three-Act Architecture**
- [ ] Setup establishes: ordinary world, protagonist, status quo, rules
- [ ] Act I **disruption** clear and consequential
- [ ] Act I **decision point** commits protagonist (no retreat)
- [ ] Act II **complications** escalate logically
- [ ] Act II **midpoint** shifts stakes/information/direction
- [ ] Act II **crisis** creates maximum pressure
- [ ] Act III **climax** addresses central dramatic question
- [ ] Act III **resolution** shows new equilibrium

**Beat Sheet Compliance (Snyder)**
- [ ] Opening Image establishes tone/theme (page 1)
- [ ] Theme Stated (usually by page 5)
- [ ] Setup complete by page 10
- [ ] Catalyst/Inciting Incident by page 12
- [ ] Debate (should I go?) by page 25
- [ ] Break into Two (decision to act) by page 25
- [ ] B Story (love story/relationship) begins page 30
- [ ] Fun and Games (promise of premise) pages 30-55
- [ ] Midpoint (false victory or false defeat) page 55
- [ ] Bad Guys Close In pages 55-75
- [ ] All Is Lost moment page 75
- [ ] Dark Night of the Soul pages 75-85
- [ ] Break into Three (solution) page 85
- [ ] Finale pages 85-110
- [ ] Final Image (inverse of opening) page 110

**Sequence Structure** (7-8 sequences of 12-15 pages each)
- [ ] Each sequence has **mini-arc** (setup/conflict/resolution)
- [ ] Sequences end with **escalation** or **revelation**
- [ ] Sequence transitions demonstrate **causal logic**

**Causal Chain Integrity**
- [ ] Every major plot point **caused by** previous action
- [ ] Protagonist's **decisions drive** 75%+ of plot progression
- [ ] **Coincidence** limited to inciting incident only
- [ ] **Deus ex machina** absent (solutions earned through setup)

### Character Checklist (Truby, Seger, McKee)

**Protagonist Requirements**

**Core Character Architecture**
- [ ] **Want** (conscious goal) clearly defined
- [ ] **Need** (unconscious requirement) implied or stated
- [ ] **Ghost** (formative wound) shapes current behavior
- [ ] **Moral weakness** creates internal conflict
- [ ] **Strengths** established (competency demonstrated)

**Character Function Tests**
- [ ] Protagonist makes ≥3 **major decisions** driving plot
- [ ] Protagonist demonstrates **agency** (not reactive victim)
- [ ] Protagonist **capable** of achieving goal (competent)
- [ ] Protagonist **flawed** enough to need transformation
- [ ] Protagonist **relatable** (audience can empathize)

**Character Arc Validation (Truby's 7-arc-steps)**
- [ ] **Weakness/Need**: Moral/psychological problem established
- [ ] **Desire**: Clear goal drives story
- [ ] **Opponent**: Antagonist provides authentic opposition
- [ ] **Plan**: Strategy to achieve goal
- [ ] **Battle**: Escalating conflict with opponent
- [ ] **Self-Revelation**: Discovers truth about self
- [ ] **New Equilibrium**: Demonstrates transformation or steadfastness

**Antagonist Requirements**

**Opposition Quality**
- [ ] Antagonist has **valid goal** (not evil for evil's sake)
- [ ] Antagonist **capable** of defeating protagonist
- [ ] Antagonist **actively pursues** own agenda
- [ ] Antagonist and protagonist have **conflicting values**
- [ ] Antagonist forces protagonist to **make hard choices**

**Supporting Cast Functions**
- [ ] **Ally/Sidekick**: Provides support and perspective
- [ ] **Mentor**: Guides protagonist (then becomes unavailable)
- [ ] **Love Interest**: Tests protagonist's values
- [ ] **Tempter**: Offers false solution
- [ ] **Skeptic**: Voices audience doubts

**Character Consistency Verification (Seger)**

**Behavioral Consistency Test**
- [ ] Character decisions **consistent** with established values
- [ ] Speech patterns **distinctive** and maintained
- [ ] Reactions **proportional** to character psychology
- [ ] Changes in behavior **motivated** by events/revelations
- [ ] **Consistency score** ≥0.75 (see Part 7 Protocol 3)

**Subtext Verification**

**Conflict Scene Dialogue** (≥60% subtext required)
- [ ] Characters **speak around** central tension
- [ ] **Gap** between what's said and what's meant
- [ ] **Emotional truth** beneath surface text
- [ ] Audience can **infer** real feelings/intentions

**Efficiency Test (Seger)**
- [ ] Could delete 25-30% of dialogue without losing essential meaning
- [ ] **Compression ratio** ≤0.33 words per scene page
- [ ] **Redundancy** ≤10% (information repeated unnecessarily)
- [ ] **Exposition** delivered obliquely (not direct statements)

**Voice Differentiation (McKee, Seger)**

**Character-Specific Markers**
- [ ] **Vocabulary** level varies by character (education/background)
- [ ] **Sentence structure** distinctive (short vs complex patterns)
- [ ] **Rhythm** distinguishable (staccato vs flowing)
- [ ] **Psychology** evident in speech (optimist vs pessimist)

**Functional Dialogue Test**
- [ ] Each line serves ≥2 functions (character + plot, character + theme, etc.)
- [ ] **Conflict** present in 70%+ of dialogue exchanges
- [ ] **Interruptions** used strategically (not constantly)
- [ ] **Silence** (reaction beats) creates space for emotion

### Scene Checklist (McKee, Field, Chitlik)

**Scene Energy Verification (McKee)**

**ΔValue Test** (every scene must change emotional charge)
- [ ] Opening value (+ or -) clearly established
- [ ] Closing value **different** from opening value
- [ ] Change occurs through **action/decision** (not time passage)
- [ ] **ΔValue ≠ 0** for ≥95% of scenes

**Scene Purpose Classification**
- [ ] **Setup**: Establishes information for later payoff
- [ ] **Development**: Advances plot or character arc
- [ ] **Payoff**: Delivers on earlier setup
- [ ] **Pivot**: Changes story direction
- [ ] Every scene falls into **one primary category**

**Scene Efficiency Audit**

**Length Optimization**
- [ ] Average scene length ≤3 pages
- [ ] Scenes >3 pages **justified** by complexity
- [ ] **Late entry/early exit** principle applied (Field)
- [ ] Excess exposition **trimmed**

**Scene Function Checklist**
- [ ] **Advances** plot OR character arc OR theme (minimum one)
- [ ] **Conflict** present (wants in opposition)
- [ ] **Emotional movement** occurs (ΔEmotion ≥0.5)
- [ ] **Visual storytelling** prioritized over dialogue

### Theme Checklist (Truby, McKee, Seger)

**Thematic Clarity Verification**

**Controlling Idea Definition (McKee)**
- [ ] Theme expressible in **single sentence** (Value + Cause)
- [ ] Example: "Love prevails when we risk vulnerability" 
- [ ] **Positive** or **negative** charge clear
- [ ] Theme **proven** through protagonist's journey

**Thematic Expression Techniques (Truby)**

**Four-Corner Opposition** (theme explored from multiple angles)
- [ ] **Protagonist** embodies one thematic position
- [ ] **Antagonist** embodies opposing position
- [ ] **Ally** offers variation on protagonist's position
- [ ] **Skeptic/Tempter** offers third/fourth perspective

**Symbolic/Motif Reinforcement**
- [ ] **Visual motifs** repeat with variation (image systems)
- [ ] **Verbal motifs** echo thematically (repeated phrases/concepts)
- [ ] **Symbolic objects** accrue meaning through story
- [ ] **Parallel situations** illuminate theme from different angles

**Theme Integration Test (Seger)**

**Organic Expression Standards**
- [ ] Theme emerges from **action** (not stated directly)
- [ ] Characters **unaware** of thematic significance (no speechifying)
- [ ] **Θ score** (theme alignment) ≥0.85 across scenes
- [ ] Theme provides **unity** without being heavy-handed

## 4. Scene-Level Quality Verification {#scene-verification}

### Individual Scene Diagnostic (McKee, Field, Chitlik)

**5-Minute Scene Quality Test**

**Energy Assessment** (McKee's Value Change)
1. What is the **opening value** (+ or -)?
2. What is the **closing value** (+ or -)?
3. Did the value **change**? (If no → flag for revision)
4. Is the change **significant** enough? (ΔValue > 0.3)
5. Did change occur through **action/choice** (not passive time)?

**Purpose Verification**
1. Can you identify scene's **primary purpose** in one word?
   - Setup / Development / Payoff / Pivot
2. Does scene **advance** plot, character, or theme?
3. Could scene be **cut** without story confusion? (If yes → cut or strengthen)
4. Does scene contain **new information** (no redundancy)?
5. Is scene **visually interesting** (not just talking heads)?

**Efficiency Check**
1. Could scene **start later** (later entry point)?
2. Could scene **end earlier** (earlier exit point)?
3. Is every line of dialogue **necessary**?
4. Could any action be **shown visually** instead of discussed?
5. Is scene length ≤3 pages (or justified if longer)?

**Conflict Verification**
1. Are **wants in opposition** (clear conflict)?
2. Does conflict **escalate** through scene?
3. Is conflict **specific** to these characters?
4. Does conflict create **emotional stakes**?
5. Does resolution create **new complications** (momentum)?

**Scene Health Score Calculation**

| Category | Weight | Checkmarks | Score |
|----------|--------|------------|-------|
| Energy Assessment | 30% | ___/5 | ___% |
| Purpose Verification | 25% | ___/5 | ___% |
| Efficiency Check | 20% | ___/5 | ___% |
| Conflict Verification | 25% | ___/5 | ___% |
| **Total Scene Health** | | | **___/100** |

**Scene Health Thresholds:**
- **90-100**: Excellent scene, minimal revision needed
- **75-89**: Good scene, minor efficiency improvements possible
- **60-74**: Adequate scene, significant revision recommended
- **<60**: Weak scene, major restructuring or deletion required

## 5. Professional Readiness Assessment {#professional-readiness}

### Industry Standard Verification (Seger, Snyder, Gordon)

**Format Excellence Checklist**

**Technical Format**
- [ ] Proper scene heading format (INT./EXT. LOCATION - TIME)
- [ ] Consistent slug line formatting throughout
- [ ] Character names **CAPITALIZED** in dialogue blocks
- [ ] Character names capitalized on **first appearance** in action
- [ ] Parentheticals used sparingly and correctly
- [ ] Transitions (CUT TO:, DISSOLVE TO:) used appropriately (or omitted per modern style)
- [ ] Page numbers present and accurate
- [ ] No manual page breaks (natural pagination)

**Title Page Standards**
- [ ] Title **centered**, capitalized
- [ ] "Written by" **centered** below title
- [ ] Author name **centered** below byline
- [ ] Contact information **lower left** or **lower right**
- [ ] Draft date/version number present
- [ ] Copyright notice (if applicable)
- [ ] **NO**: WGA registration numbers, artwork, quotes, extraneous text

**Professional Presentation**
- [ ] Courier 12pt font
- [ ] Standard 1.5" left margin, 1" other margins
- [ ] Consistent spacing (no double-spaces or formatting inconsistencies)
- [ ] Clean, professional appearance
- [ ] No coffee stains, wrinkles, or physical damage (if printed)

**Completion Verification**

**Page Count Standards by Genre**
- [ ] **Feature Drama**: 100-120 pages
- [ ] **Feature Comedy**: 90-110 pages
- [ ] **Feature Action**: 100-120 pages
- [ ] **Feature Thriller**: 90-110 pages
- [ ] **TV Drama Hour**: 55-65 pages
- [ ] **TV Comedy Half-Hour**: 25-35 pages

**Story Completeness**
- [ ] All **plot threads** resolved (or deliberately left open)
- [ ] All **major questions** answered
- [ ] No **unintentional loose ends**
- [ ] **Character arcs** complete (or positioned for sequel)

**Error-Free Guarantee**
- [ ] **Zero typos** (proofread minimum 3 times)
- [ ] **Zero formatting errors** verified

- [ ] **Timeline continuity** verified (no day/night inconsistencies)

## 6. Genre-Specific Quality Markers {#genre-markers}

### Comedy Quality Standards (Vorhaus, Blake, Rosenfield)

**Structural Comedy Requirements**

**Beat Density Index** (Blake)
- [ ] **Setup** every 2-3 pages
- [ ] **Punchline** (payoff) following setup
- [ ] **Callback** reinforcing earlier jokes
- [ ] Average **6-8 laugh moments** per 10 pages

**Tonal Consistency**
- [ ] Comedy **register** consistent (farce vs romantic vs dark)
- [ ] **Heart beats** integrated (emotional grounding)
- [ ] **Character comedy** (not just situation comedy)
- [ ] **Rule of Three** applied to escalation patterns

**Comedy-Specific Scene Tests**
- [ ] Scenes **escalate** comedically (mounting chaos or revelations)
- [ ] **Characters behave seriously** within absurd situations
- [ ] **Timing** supported by action description rhythm
- [ ] **Physical comedy** clearly staged (visual clarity)

---

### Thriller/Suspense Quality Standards (McKee, Field)

**Tension Architecture**

**Clock Pressure**
- [ ] **Deadline** established and reinforced
- [ ] **Time compression** increases urgency
- [ ] **Ticking clock** visible to audience (countdown elements)

**Information Control**
- [ ] Audience knows **≥ protagonist** (dramatic irony) OR
- [ ] Audience knows **< protagonist** (mystery mode)
- [ ] **Reveals** timed for maximum impact
- [ ] **Red herrings** deployed strategically

**Thriller-Specific Scene Requirements**
- [ ] **Escalating** jeopardy/stakes
- [ ] **Complications** prevent easy solutions
- [ ] **Reversals** subvert expectations
- [ ] **Atmospheric** tension maintained

---

### Drama Quality Standards (Seger, McKee, Truby)

**Emotional Truth Verification**

**Character Authenticity**
- [ ] Emotional responses **proportional** and **authentic**
- [ ] Moral choices **difficult** and **consequential**
- [ ] **Internal conflict** as important as external conflict
- [ ] **Transformation** earned through suffering/revelation

**Thematic Depth**
- [ ] **Complex questions** explored (no easy answers)
- [ ] **Multiple perspectives** on theme
- [ ] **Moral ambiguity** where appropriate
- [ ] **Emotional resonance** over action spectacle

---

### Action Quality Standards (Field, Snyder)

**Physical Storytelling Excellence**

**Action Clarity**
- [ ] **Geography** of action clearly established
- [ ] **Stakes** of each action beat clear
- [ ] **Choreography** comprehensible on page
- [ ] **Cause/effect** logic maintained

**Escalation Pattern**
- [ ] Action sequences **escalate** in intensity
- [ ] **Complications** prevent easy victories
- [ ] **Character choices** drive action (not random chaos)
- [ ] **Emotional stakes** present (not just physical)

## 8. Final Polish Protocols {#final-polish}

### Micro-Level Excellence Checklist (Seger, Chitlik)

**Action Line Polish**

**Visual Specificity**
- [ ] **Shows** rather than tells (visual description)
- [ ] **Active verbs** predominate (punches, not hits)
- [ ] **Specific details** create vivid images
- [ ] **Economy** achieved (3-4 line maximum per paragraph)

**Readability Optimization**
- [ ] White space adequate (not dense text blocks)
- [ ] **Varied paragraph length** (visual rhythm)
- [ ] **Present tense** maintained consistently
- [ ] **Smooth reading flow** (no awkward phrasing)

**Dialogue Polish**

**Final Dialogue Pass Checklist**
- [ ] Could delete **25%** without losing essential meaning (efficiency test)
- [ ] Character voices **remain distinctive** throughout
- [ ] **Subtext** present in ≥60% of conflict dialogue
- [ ] **Rhythm variation** between characters (musical quality)
- [ ] **Naturalistic elements** present (interruptions, ellipses, pauses)
- [ ] **Exposition** delivered obliquely (no info dumps)

**Grammar and Mechanics**

**Zero-Error Standard**
- [ ] Spell-checked **three times** (computer + manual)
- [ ] Grammar verified (proper punctuation, no run-ons)
- [ ] Homophone check (their/there, your/you're, its/it's)
- [ ] Consistent verb tense throughout
- [ ] Proper capitalization (character names, sounds, first appearances)

**Format Consistency**

**Final Format Audit**
- [ ] All scene headings **identical format**
- [ ] Character names **spelled consistently** (including minor characters)
- [ ] Parentheticals **used sparingly** and correctly
- [ ] Page breaks **natural** (no awkward dialogue splits)
- [ ] Margins **consistent** throughout

### Final Read-Through Protocol

**The Three-Pass Polish System (Seger, Chitlik)**

**Pass 1: Read for Story**
- **Purpose**: Experience script as audience would
- **Method**: Read straight through without stopping
- **Note**: Flag moments that confuse or bore (don't fix yet)
- **Outcome**: Identify macro-level issues

**Pass 2: Read for Craft**
- **Purpose**: Verify technical excellence
- **Method**: Slow read, checking structure/character/dialogue systems
- **Note**: Mark specific technical issues
- **Outcome**: Create targeted fix list

**Pass 3: Read for Polish**
- **Purpose**: Catch micro-level errors
- **Method**: Word-by-word attention to language
- **Note**: Fix typos, awkward phrasing, format errors
- **Outcome**: Error-free manuscript

**Alternative: Professional Reading**
- **Read aloud**: Catches awkward dialogue and rhythm issues
- **Table read**: Hear actors perform dialogue (reveals unnatural language)
- **Fresh eyes**: Give to trusted reader for feedback
- **Rest period**: Let script sit 1-2 weeks before final pass
