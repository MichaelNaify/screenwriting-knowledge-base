§2 COGNITIVE MECHANICS — THE MIND OF STRUCTURE

2.1 The Story–Mind Equation (#cognitive-mirror)

Field’s model assumes that screenplays mirror cognition: human beings think in acts.
When confronted by change, the brain performs a tri-phasic sequence — orientation → problem-solving → integration.
Field’s setup / confrontation / resolution thus describes the mental rhythm of understanding itself.

Cognitive Phase	Story Act	Brain Operation	Output
Orientation	Act I	Perceptual mapping / expectation formation	Context
Disruption	Act II	Working-memory strain / pattern testing	Meaning-in-progress
Integration	Act III	Schema update / emotional closure	Insight

By aligning narrative rhythm with neural rhythm, Field ensures that story comprehension feels inevitable.
The viewer’s pre-frontal cortex predicts outcomes at the same rate the screenplay supplies reversals, producing immersion.

⸻

2.2 Plot Points as Neural Triggers (#event-threshold)

Plot points operate as prediction-error spikes — moments when the audience’s model of the story fails, forcing recalibration.
Neuroscientifically, they release dopamine, maintaining attention.
Field’s insistence that the first plot point occur by ≈ page 30 is therefore not arbitrary but biologically synchronized: at ≈ 25 minutes, attention decay begins unless disrupted.

Thus, the “Plot Point” equals a controlled cognitive jolt that refreshes engagement.
The “Midpoint Reversal” doubles this function: it inverts prediction direction and renews suspense.

⸻

2.3 Character Arc and Mirror Neurons (#empathy-system)

Field’s dramatic need creates empathic coupling.
When a protagonist acts under pressure, mirror-neuron circuits simulate the same emotion in the viewer.
Transformation occurs when observed behavior resolves internal contradiction, giving the audience a vicarious sense of growth.

Principle: The arc is not just plot; it is the viewer’s emotional learning curve.

Stage 6 maps this to a function:

Empathy(t) = ∂Identification / ∂Obstacle

As obstacle intensity rises, identification derivative increases; too flat a curve (weak conflict) = boredom.
Field’s “progressive complication” rule thus optimizes empathy gradient management.

⸻

2.4 Scene Design and Working Memory (#chunking-law)

Human working memory can hold ≈ 7 ± 2 information chunks.
Field’s scene units correspond almost exactly: each scene delivers one new idea, emotion, or reversal.
Sequences (2–5 scenes) form macro-chunks the brain can grasp as story patterns.
Hence Field’s “Sequence Method” (≈ 12 per feature film) emerges as a psychological optimization of working memory capacity.

⸻

2.5 The Tension–Relief Oscillator (#emotional-wave)

Field implicitly teaches that suspense is a waveform.
Each beat of setup–payoff creates oscillation between stress and release.
The frequency of these waves defines genre identity:
	•	High frequency → Action / Thriller
	•	Low frequency → Drama / Romance
	•	Variable frequency → Comedy (#timing-logic)

Writers modulate amplitude (stakes) and period (scene length) to match expected audience resonance.
Field’s method therefore functions as primitive emotional signal engineering.
