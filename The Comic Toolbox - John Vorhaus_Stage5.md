STAGE 5 — CHUNK A

Operational Philosophy | The Writer as System

⸻

§0  Purpose and Orientation

Stage 5 marks the moment when the comic writer ceases to study humor and begins to run humor as a system.
The previous stages have each delivered a different layer of intelligence:

Stage	Function	Output
1	Analytical Decomposition	Breakdown of tools and terminology of comedy
2	Theoretical Synthesis	Integration of principles across narrative and psychology
3	Epistemic Architecture	Comedy as knowledge system and cognitive discipline
4	Applied Trueform	Operational use of comic principles in story design
5	Production Engine	Continuous generation of comic material at professional volume

The goal of Stage 5 is to transform John Vorhaus’s Comic Toolbox into a repeatable creative operating system—a closed-loop mechanism that transforms inputs (ideas, observations, situations) into outputs (jokes, scenes, scripts) with measurable efficiency and emotional truth.

⸻

§1  From Mindset to Mechanism

Vorhaus’s original toolbox framed comedy as craft: a set of tools anyone can learn.
Stage 5 advances this stance: craft must run itself.
The writer is no longer a person attempting humor but an operating system for comic perception.

Core premises:
	1.	Everything is a System. Joke, scene, character, and theme are not isolated; they share inputs and outputs in a causal network.
	2.	Systems learn through iteration. Each draft, pitch, and performance feeds back data to improve pattern recognition.
	3.	Systemic humor emerges from tension and release. The writer must measure and manage that tension as energy.
	4.	Automation follows mastery. Tools used consciously in Stage 4 must become unconscious protocols in Stage 5.
	5.	Quality equals throughput × learning rate. A comic system that produces more material and learns faster outpaces mere talent.

The writer at Stage 5 no longer asks, “Am I funny?” but instead tracks: “Is my system functioning?”

⸻

§2  The Operational Identity of the Comic Writer

In the Stage 5 model, identity is redefined as workflow.
The comic writer is a multi-layer machine composed of distinct yet interlocking modules:

Layer	Function	Observable Output
Perceptual Layer	Scans the world for incongruity (#pattern-recognition #irony)	Idea capture log
Cognitive Layer	Transforms raw observations into premise statements	Beat notes / comic premises
Structural Layer	Applies setup/payoff, reversal, and rule-of-three mechanics	Scenes and jokes in draft
Affective Layer	Manages tone and empathy (#emotional-geometry)	Audience engagement
Reflective Layer	Analyzes feedback, edits system parameters	Revised workflow

This architecture converts the Vorhaus axiom “comedy is truth and pain” into a programmable formula:

Truth × Pain → Laughter = Empathy + Surprise / Distance.

Every module works to maintain that balance.

⸻

§3  Systemic Purpose: From Art to Throughput

The comic system exists to produce value: scripts, scenes, and moments that generate reliable laughter and emotional connection.
Stage 5 reframes the writer’s practice through four metrics:

Metric	Definition	Function
Throughput	Volume of viable material per cycle (day/week)	Encourages quantity for pattern emergence
Conversion Rate	% of raw ideas that evolve into performable scenes	Measures system efficiency
Resonance Index	Audience response score across tests (live or script reads)	Quantifies emotional impact
Learning Velocity	Rate at which new lessons alter next output	Ensures adaptability

These metrics mirror industrial production without killing artistic spontaneity; they merely translate intuition into data.

⸻

§4  The Vorhaus Doctrine in Systemic Form

Vorhaus argues that comedy is “the juxtaposition of truth and incongruity.”
Stage 5 turns that doctrine into an operational algorithm:
	1.	Scan Reality → Collect truth statements (observation phase).
	2.	Introduce Incongruity → Twist the expected logic.
	3.	Amplify Tension → Heighten stakes or contrast.
	4.	Deliver Recognition → Audience realizes their own pattern error.
	5.	Resolve Emotionally → Return to truth with changed perspective.

Each cycle completes a unit of humor production.
Writers run dozens of these micro-loops per session.
When automated, they form the “humor engine” at the core of the Stage 5 system.

⸻

§5  Inputs, Processes, Outputs — The Production Loop

Input Domains
	•	Observation (#life-data)
	•	Language (#idiom #metaphor #banal-detail)
	•	Memory (#personal-pain #failure-archive)
	•	Cultural noise (#zeitgeist #absurdity)

Process Modules

Outputs

	•	Scene units (#comic-beat #reversal #setup-payoff)
	•	Script segments (#sequence architecture)
	•	Pedagogical artifacts (#lecture #manual #bootcamp module)

⸻

§6  Behavioral Reprogramming — Becoming Your Own Machine

The transition to systemic operation requires behavioral conditioning.
Writers must develop habits that replace willpower with design:
	1.	Daily Cycle Activation → Fixed time slots for input/output logging.
	2.	Latency Tolerance → Accept the time gap between effort and result.
	3.	Feedback Addiction → Crave data from readers more than praise.
	4.	Iterative Faith → Assume improvement is a mathematical certainty.
	5.	Failure Integration → Treat bombed jokes as essential training data.

By replacing ego with iteration, the writer achieves the state Vorhaus calls “joyful play under pressure.”

⸻

§7  Energy Economy of Comedy

All systems require fuel.
For the comic writer, fuel = psychic energy derived from emotion, curiosity, and risk.
Stage 5 establishes an energy management model:

Source	Positive Charge	Negative Drain	Control Mechanism
Curiosity	Generates inputs	Burnout from over-consumption	Scheduled in-take windows
Emotion	Adds authentic stakes	Exhaustion from over-identification	Emotional rotation method
Community	Provides feedback loops	Group-think risk	Diversity of test audiences
Routine	Sustains output	Creative boredom	Periodic pattern breaks

The writer’s goal is not constant inspiration but stable energy flow—humor as a renewable resource.

⸻

§8  Philosophical Coda — The Writer as Systemic Consciousness

The Stage 5 comic writer does not merely produce humor; they embody the logic of humor itself.
They understand that the world is a data stream of incongruity waiting to be patterned into joy.
They accept that laughter is information shared between minds, and their role is to engineer that signal with precision and love.

When the system runs smoothly, ego and process disappear; only the flow of comic energy remains.

Stage 5 mantra: You are the toolbox. Run yourself well.

CHUNK B — System Architecture and Workflow Design

⸻

§1  The Architecture of Comic Production

At Stage 5, the writer no longer faces the blank page but runs a machine whose subsystems deliver continuous material.
The architecture functions as both an industrial assembly line and a creative bioreactor—balancing mechanical reliability with organic spontaneity.

System Level	Description	Governing Principle
Macro-System	Yearly & quarterly planning cycles; long-form goals (screenplays, pilots, novels)	Strategic direction
Meso-System	Weekly sprints and thematic clusters (premise packets, character labs)	Operational throughput
Micro-System	Daily idea generation, joke writing, editing loops	Tactical rhythm
Nano-System	Moment-to-moment cognitive micro-habits	Reflexive mastery

All layers synchronize via feedback timing, the fundamental control variable. A system that measures its output in fixed intervals learns faster than one that waits for inspiration.

⸻

§2  The Five-Cycle Model

The Comic Toolbox Production Engine operates through five interdependent cycles.
Each cycle corresponds to one cognitive function and one physical practice.

Cycle	Cognitive Function	Primary Task	Output
1. Observation	Pattern detection	Record incongruities, overheard phrases, contradictions	Raw idea list
2. Transformation	Analogical mapping	Convert observations into “comic premises”	Premise log
3. Construction	Structural assembly	Build setups, reversals, payoffs	Scene drafts
4. Calibration	Testing and modulation	Table reads, AI test laughter, audience notes	Revised draft
5. Renewal	System reflection	Analyze data, schedule restock of ideas	Updated process map

The cycle is fractal—each phase appears within every scale of work.
A joke follows the same rhythm as a screenplay.

⸻

§3  The Daily Production Loop

The daily loop ensures steady comedic throughput and resistance to burnout.
It transforms abstract productivity into measurable creative cadence.

Standard Loop (approx. 5 hours total)
	1.	Warm-Up (30 min) – Wordplay drills, inversion exercises, improvisational free-writes (#linguistic-agility).
	2.	Idea Harvest (45 min) – Review observation logs, headlines, and memory fragments. Tag ten potential premises.
	3.	Premise Expansion (90 min) – Build short sketches or two-beat dialogues for top three ideas.
	4.	Systemic Drafting (90 min) – Integrate one premise into a scene or episode outline using Vorhaus’s setup/payoff grid.
	5.	Reflection & Metrics (25 min) – Record counts: ideas → jokes → pages → iterations. Note anomalies or new rules.

Each day produces data as well as text. Over time, this data becomes predictive—revealing personal tempo, fatigue thresholds, and peak creativity windows.

“Professional funny is predictable funny.” — Stage 5 maxim

⸻

§4  Weekly Sprint Protocol

A week in the comic system functions like a miniature season of a show: planning, generation, revision, and retrospective.
The sprint imposes constructive deadlines without punishing spontaneity.

Day	Focus	Deliverable	Feedback
Mon	Data review + target reset	Week’s “north-star” theme (e.g., absurd honesty)	self-audit
Tue–Wed	Intensive idea generation	20 new premises + 4 scene stubs	peer test
Thu	Assembly day	1 completed scene or sequence	internal read
Fri	Diagnostic review	Tag top 3 “keeper” bits	system log update
Sat	Long-form integration	Add best material to ongoing script	hybrid peer/AI feedback
Sun	Rest → absorb → refuel	Curated media watchlist	passive learning

Sprints create temporal pressure, Vorhaus’s “constraint of comedy.” Pressure yields choice; choice yields style.

⸻

§5  Monthly and Quarterly Scaling

At higher temporal levels, the system must align with industry realities—deadlines, competitions, and seasonal markets.

Monthly Review Checklist
	•	80 hours of logged creative work
	•	1 short script or equivalent 25 pages
	•	Updated idea database (≥ 300 entries)
	•	Performance metrics: conversion %, laughter score, turnaround time

Quarterly Objectives
	•	One polished spec or pilot draft
	•	Portfolio recalibration (genres, tones)
	•	External testing: festival submissions, open-mic read, online release
	•	Self-evaluation: “What pattern of success should I repeat?”

This temporal architecture converts the chaos of inspiration into a rhythmic business model.

      ↓

§7  Tools and Automation Protocols

Stage 5 embraces assistive automation without outsourcing creativity.
Software and structured templates free cognitive bandwidth for comedic leaps.

Tool Type	Function	Example Implementation
Capture Systems	Rapid idea logging	Voice-to-text mobile app
Sorting Algorithms	Categorize premises by tone or archetype	Tag parser (e.g., “#sarcasm #status #pain”)

§8  Collaboration Layer — Distributed Comedy Systems

No system scales in isolation.
The Stage 5 writer integrates into a networked creative ecosystem—rooms, partners, or online cohorts.

Collaboration Architecture

Node	Purpose	Governance Rule
Writers’ Micro-Room (3-6 people)	Idea exchange, feedback	“Yes, and…” only
Beta Readers Pool	External audience testing	Diversity = data variety
AI Simulation Layer	Rapid tone analysis, punch-up trials	Tool not arbiter
Producer Interface	Translation to deliverables	Convert jokes → value propositions

Each node increases information flow.
Systemic collaboration transforms humor from individual wit into collective intelligence.

⸻

§9  Temporal Compression — Maximizing Throughput

Professional comedy rewards velocity.
Stage 5 applies temporal compression to shorten feedback loops without degrading quality.

Techniques:
	1.	Parallel Drafting – Maintain two active projects (one generative, one editorial) to prevent downtime.
	2.	Time Boxing – Fixed 90-minute creative blocks; completion beats perfection.
	3.	Rolling Revision – Edit yesterday’s work first; primes brain for creation.
	4.	Version Control – Label drafts sequentially (scene_3B_funnyfix) to avoid confusion.
	5.	Data Reuse – Recycle failed jokes as setup seeds in new contexts.

Compression teaches the nervous system to equate speed with play, not panic.

⸻

§10  System Diagnostics — Feedback Architecture

A functioning system measures itself.
Diagnostics transform vague feelings (“it’s not funny”) into actionable metrics.

Diagnostic	Measurement Method	Correction Protocol
Idea Yield	# of usable premises ÷ # of captured inputs	Adjust observation breadth
Joke Efficiency	Laughs per 100 words or per page	Re-evaluate setup density
Beat Cohesion	% of beats with emotional payoff	Add connective reversals
Tone Drift	Deviation from intended comic persona	Persona recalibration
Energy Variance	Mood / output correlation	Adjust work-rest ratio

When logged weekly, diagnostics create an adaptive dashboard—a live self-portrait of the writer’s evolving comedic fingerprint.

⸻

§11  The Workflow as Identity

Through constant iteration, the writer’s workflow becomes their signature.
Rhythm replaces style; procedure becomes voice.
Each practitioner’s system stabilizes around unique constants:
	•	Preferred incongruity domains (language / behavior / logic)
	•	Typical escalation rate (slow irony ↔ rapid absurdity)
	•	Emotional baseline (hopeful ↔ cynical)
	•	Structural bias (dialogue ↔ visual)

Recognizing these constants allows targeted evolution rather than random change.
Stage 5 treats identity not as mystery but as calibrated algorithm.

CHUNK C — Cognitive Mechanics | Ideation → Execution Loop

⸻

§1 | The Cognitive Architecture of Comic Creation

Stage 5 reframes “being funny” as a predictable cognitive sequence.
Every joke, beat, or comic moment passes through the same mental pipeline:

Phase	Cognitive Action	Outcome
1. Input Recognition	Notice an incongruity or violation of expectation	Seed idea
2. Emotional Tagging	Attach truth and pain (#authenticity)	Empathic charge
3. Transformation	Apply contrast, exaggeration, or reversal	Comedic potential
4. Structuring	Order beats for maximum tension/release	Setup–Payoff model
5. Expression	Render via language or action	Executable bit
6. Evaluation	Test for surprise + recognition	Iteration data

By automating these six moves, the writer transforms intuition into a repeatable cognitive rhythm.

⸻

§2 | The Neural Triad: Surprise, Empathy, Precision

Comedy activates a triangular loop in the brain:
	1.	Surprise (Hippocampus / Pattern Error) — detects deviation.
	2.	Empathy (Limbic System) — processes the pain or truth.
	3.	Precision (Prefrontal Cortex) — crafts language and timing.

The professional humorist runs this triad deliberately.
Each subsystem has its exercise:

Subsystem	Training Exercise	Purpose
Surprise	“Wrong-Answer Drills” – respond to questions with absurd logic	Loosen pattern rigidity
Empathy	“Pain Inventory” – catalogue embarrassments, failures	Source emotional fuel
Precision	“Compression Pass” – edit jokes to ≤ 12 words	Sharpen delivery

Regular cycling through the triad sustains comic reflexes like an athlete’s muscle memory.

⸻

§3 | The Ideation Matrix

Vorhaus’s “comic premise” = Truth + Pain × Incongruity.
Stage 5 operationalizes it through a four-quadrant ideation grid:

Quadrant	Stimulus Source	Cognitive Prompt	Example
A – Reality Bend	Everyday truths	“What if the normal rule reversed?”	“Honesty app that destroys relationships.”
B – Emotional Echo	Personal pain	“What hurts but is ridiculous?”	“Therapist ghosted me for self-care.”
C – Cultural Lens	Social absurdities	“What do we pretend is normal?”	“Office fire-drill for feelings.”
D – Formal Play	Language/structure	“How can the form misbehave?”	Haiku rant about parking tickets.

During daily loops (#see Chunk B §3), the writer spends one hour rotating through these quadrants to ensure ideational diversity.

⸻

§4 | From Premise to Beat: The Execution Algorithm

Once a premise exists, Stage 5 converts it into beats via the Execution Algorithm:

Premise → Promise → Escalation → Collapse → Aftermath

Step	Action	Cognitive Goal
Premise	Define comic truth	Clarity
Promise	Signal audience expectation	Set up pattern
Escalation	Intensify contradiction	Increase tension
Collapse	Deliver reversal	Trigger surprise
Aftermath	Restore new truth	Empathic closure

Every beat must move the system forward—either by heightening tension or releasing it.
If neither occurs, the beat is waste.

⸻

§5 | Cognitive Load Management

Humor writing is a series of controlled micro-stress events.
Too much cognitive load → over-thinking → dead jokes.
Stage 5 prescribes a three-zone management protocol:

Zone	Load Level	Optimal Task	Duration
Green	Low focus	Free-association / brainstorm	30–45 min
Yellow	Moderate focus	Scene construction	90 min
Red	High focus	Revision & punch-up	30 min max

Cycle Green → Yellow → Red → break → reset.
Maintaining rhythm prevents humor fatigue and keeps spontaneity alive.

⸻

§6 | Attentional Switching – Macro & Micro Humor Modes

Vorhaus distinguishes between macro story humor (theme, plot) and micro beat humor (line, gesture).
Stage 5 codifies these as attention modes:

Mode	Focus Span	Question	Desired Signal
Macro Mode	5–20 pages	“What truth am I testing?”	Emotional continuity
Micro Mode	1–5 lines	“What surprise am I delivering?”	Immediate laugh pulse

Expert writers oscillate between modes every few minutes. The Stage 5 system uses visual markers in drafts (#macro / #micro) to train this switching reflex.

⸻

§7 | Pattern Recognition and Distortion

Humor lives in pattern recognition and its corruption.
The system therefore tracks three pattern types:
	1.	Structural Pattern: rule of three, reversal, callback.
	2.	Semantic Pattern: wordplay, double meaning, misdirection.
	3.	Behavioral Pattern: status shift, emotional flip, timing error.

Each pattern has a distortion vector that generates the laugh:

Pattern → Expectation → Violation → Recognition → Resolution

Training tip: collect pattern examples from stand-up, sitcoms, TikToks, and tag each step.
After 100 cases, the brain begins auto-detecting opportunities for violation—automation achieved.

⸻

§8 | The Emotion-to-Language Converter

Laughter is bodily; writing is verbal. The Stage 5 system bridges them through the Emotion-to-Language Converter:
	1.	Identify core feeling (shame, envy, hope).
	2.	Locate its contradiction (why it’s funny or painful).
	3.	Translate to metaphor or gesture.
	4.	Compress to punch line.

Example:
Feeling = inadequacy → Contradiction = boast about failure → Language = “I’m a self-made disaster.”

This conversion turns private emotion into public signal without exposure trauma—humor as emotional encryption.

⸻

§9 | Memory Architecture and Idea Storage

Human memory is a poor filing system. The Stage 5 writer builds an external “comic RAM”:

Repository	Format	Use
Idea Bank	Short phrases / premises	Seed input
Pattern Library	Examples tagged by mechanic	Training data
Pain Archive	Anecdotes of failure / embarrassment	Emotional fuel
Dialogue Scraps	Unassigned lines	Plug-and-play repository

Every 90 days, the system cleans and re-indexes these archives.
Information decay is prevented; freshness stays high.

⸻

§10 | Feedback Integration — Neural Learning Loop

Cognition improves only when data returns to the system.
The Neural Learning Loop formalizes feedback as algorithm:

Trial → Feedback → Analysis → Adjustment → Retrial

Each step is logged numerically: laugh count, duration, energy drop.
Writers graph these results to visualize humor efficiency over time.
Iteration speed = learning velocity. Stage 5 targets ≥ 5 cycles per bit before archiving.

⸻

§11 | Cognitive Health and Creative Neurosis Management

Comedic insight thrives on paradox: the writer must stay both wounded and functional.
Stage 5 introduces “bounded vulnerability”—controlled exposure to truth and pain without psychic bleed.

Risk	Symptom	Remedy
Over-Identification	Jokes become rants	Increase distance through metaphor
Detachment Fatigue	No emotional charge	Re-immerse via real-world experience
Cynicism Overload	Nihilism kills humor	Inject hope beats (see Stage 4 §8)

Humor is the alchemy of trauma into pattern; healthy systems guard the alchemist.

CHUNK D — Structural Dynamics | Scene & Sequence Production

⸻

§1 | The Architecture of Comic Movement

Comedy operates through energy transfer — tension builds, expectation rises, then release converts it to laughter.
At Stage 5, structural mastery becomes mechanical. Every scene must express one clean energy curve.

Phase	Energy State	Narrative Function
Setup	Low potential; order established	Establish normalcy
Complication	Rising distortion	Escalate incongruity
Crisis	Peak contradiction	Force confrontation
Payoff	Sudden release	Deliver recognition + surprise
Afterglow	Restored order (changed)	Emotional recalibration

A story, act, or feature script is a stack of these micro-curves — laughter as wave mechanics.

⸻

§2 | Scene Typologies — The Comic Building Blocks

Stage 5 categorizes every comic scene by dominant mechanic, enabling algorithmic design.

Type	Core Mechanic	Description	Example Pattern
Status Flip Scene	Power inversion	High-status character loses control	boss humiliated by intern
Escalation Scene	Repetition + intensification	Same action grows absurd	apology becomes public meltdown
Expectation Betrayal	Misdirection	Setup logic violated	serious confession turns sales pitch
Fish-Out-of-Water	Context clash	Character misreads norms	monk at karaoke night
Emotional Collision	Tone dissonance	Feelings out of sync	wedding tears meet slapstick chaos

Tagging scenes by type (e.g., #status-flip, #expectation-betrayal) creates searchable structural metadata across the script.

⸻

§3 | The Scene Engine Blueprint

Every scene must move one variable of the system:
	•	Character goal
	•	Audience expectation
	•	Thematic truth
	•	Comic tension

Scene Algorithm

INTENTION → OBSTACLE → REACTION → ESCALATION → REVERSAL → CONSEQUENCE

Step	Function	Humor Tool
Intention	Establish clear motive	Straight line (truth)
Obstacle	Introduce conflict	Incongruity
Reaction	Emotional beat	Pain / recognition
Escalation	Intensify absurdity	Rule-of-three
Reversal	Surprise or inversion	Irony / contradiction
Consequence	New equilibrium	Heart / empathy

When logged scene by scene, the algorithm yields a visual heat-map of laughter potential across acts.

⸻

§4 | The Rule-of-Three as Structural Code

The Rule-of-Three is not superstition; it is a neurological sweet spot.
The brain anticipates pattern completion at the third beat. Stage 5 translates this into timeline logic:

Beat	Function	Energy Value
1	Establish expectation	+1
2	Reinforce pattern	+2
3	Violate pattern	−3 → +release

Stacked across sequences, triples create rhythmic predictability that the writer can later fracture for shock value.

Advanced Variant – 3×3 Matrix:
Three escalating triads compose an act. The final triad collapses into emotional truth, anchoring theme.

⸻

§5 | Reversal Mathematics

Reversal = Δ (Expectation − Outcome).
High Δ = big laugh; low Δ = smile.
Stage 5 quantifies reversals by distance and direction:

Axis	Low Δ (micro)	High Δ (macro)
Logic Axis	wordplay, contradiction	premise collapse
Emotion Axis	minor embarrassment	heartbreak reframed as absurd
Status Axis	small slip	total humiliation
Reality Axis	believable error	surreal violation

Professional calibration: vary axis and amplitude every 3–5 pages to prevent pattern fatigue.

⸻

§6 | Comic Sequences — From Beat Chains to Narrative Engines

A sequence is a closed loop of 3–7 scenes unified by one escalating comic question.
Formula:
Premise → Variations → Collapse → Consequence.

Example — “Lying for Love” Sequence
	1.	Lie told (Setup)
	2.	Lie sustained (Escalation)
	3.	Lie expanded (Absurdity)
	4.	Truth discovered (Collapse)
	5.	Relationship shifts (Consequence)

By mapping sequences like modular circuits, large stories remain controllable.
Each loop becomes a reusable comedy engine transferable between projects.

⸻

§7 | Temporal Engineering — Controlling Pace and Breath

Comic rhythm alternates compression and release. Stage 5 uses time metrics to maintain audience regulation:

Unit	Optimal Duration	Purpose
Beat	≤ 10 seconds (screen time)	Surprise reflex
Scene	1–3 minutes	Build sustained tension
Sequence	5–10 minutes	Emotional payoff
Act	25–30 minutes	Thematic transformation

Editing strategy: measure average beat density per page.
Target = 3–5 laugh beats per minute without emotional exhaustion.

⸻

§8 | Spatial Design — Blocking and Visual Humor

Structure is spatial as much as temporal.
Stage 5 directs writers to storyboard each comic scene spatially:
	•	Vector Comedy: Motion drives joke (chase, fall, collision).
	•	Table Comedy: Stillness drives tension (silence, glance, reaction).
	•	Spatial Irony: Background contradicts foreground action.

Spatial tags (#vector, #table, #irony) ensure variety and visual rhythm across a screenplay.

⸻

§9 | Ensemble Mechanics — Multi-Character Systems

Comedy multiplies through interaction density.
Define each character as a vector of truth distortion:

Character Type	Function	Example
Driver	Initiates conflict	Kramer enters room

Measures skill acceleration. Target growth ≥ 5 % per cycle.

5. Sustainability Index (SI)
Composite score of sleep, mood, social contact, and output consistency.
If SI < 70 %, creativity is at risk of burnout.
