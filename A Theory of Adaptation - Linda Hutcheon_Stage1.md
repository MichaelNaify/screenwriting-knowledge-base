## A Theory of Adaptation - Linda Hutcheon (2nd Edition, 2013)

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Document Type:** Academic Monograph | Theoretical Framework  
**Primary Domain:** Adaptation Studies | Media Theory | Cultural Studies  
**Edition Analyzed:** Second Edition (2013) with Epilogue by Siobhan O'Flynn  
**Core Innovation:** Tripartite modal theory of engagement (telling, showing, interacting)  
**Paradigmatic Position:** Post-fidelity criticism, anti-hierarchical, experiential formalism

**Key Theoretical Contribution:** Hutcheon dismantles the Romantic privileging of originality by theorizing adaptation as both product (extensive transcoding) and process (creative interpretation/palimpsestic intertextuality), while introducing the revolutionary framework of three modes of engagement that transcends traditional medium-specificity debates.

### 2.1 Primary Concept Extraction

#### **#ADAPTATION-TRIPLE-DEFINITION-COMPLETE**

**Master Tag:** #Adaptation-As-Product-Process-Reception  

**Definition Tags:**
- #Adaptation-As-Product: "An announced and extensive transposition of a particular work or works" involving transcoding across or within media
- #Adaptation-As-Process: "Both (re-)interpretation and then (re-)creation" - appropriation and salvaging
- #Adaptation-As-Reception: "A form of intertextuality experienced as palimpsests through memory of other works"

**Context Tags:**
- #Post-Romantic-Cultural-Context: Late capitalism's paradoxical valuation of novelty vs. adaptation ubiquity
- #Anti-Hierarchical-Positioning: Challenges "secondary" status of adaptations
- #Cross-Disciplinary-Integration: Literary studies, film theory, game studies, new media

**Relationship Tags:**
- → #Intertextuality-Theory (Kristeva, Barthes, Bakhtin)
- → #Translation-Theory (Benjamin, Bassnett)
- → #Imitation-Tradition (Classical imitatio/aemulatio)
- → #Poststructuralist-Textuality

**Application Tags:**
- Film adaptation of literature
- Novelization of films
- Video game transpositions
- Transmedia franchising
- Opera adaptations
- Stage-to-screen and vice versa

---

#### **#THREE-MODES-OF-ENGAGEMENT-COMPLETE**

**Master Tag:** #Modal-Tripartite-Framework

**Sub-Components:**

**A. #TELLING-MODE-Complete**
- **Technical Specification:** Narrative literature, audiobooks, radio drama
- **Cognitive Process:** Imagination-driven, mental imaging, sequential gathering of narrative details
- **Material Constraints:** Black marks on white page, aural narration
- **Immersion Type:** Imaginative world-building unconstrained by visual/aural limits
- **Temporal Control:** Reader controls pacing, can re-read, skip ahead
- **Strengths:** Interiority, point of view flexibility, irony, ambiguity, metaphor
- **Limitations:** No direct perceptual experience, requires literacy/language competence

**B. #SHOWING-MODE-Complete**
- **Technical Specification:** Film, theatre, opera, dance, television, musical performance
- **Cognitive Process:** Perception-driven, simultaneous multi-sensory decoding
- **Material Constraints:** Linear forward-driving temporality, collaborative creation
- **Immersion Type:** Perceptual (Renaissance perspective, Baroque trompe l'oeil analogy)
- **Temporal Control:** Fixed duration, controlled by creators/performers
- **Strengths:** Exteriority, spectacle, aural/visual richness, embodied performance
- **Limitations:** Interiority challenges, reduced control for audience, expensive production

**C. #INTERACTING-MODE-Complete**
- **Technical Specification:** Video games, hypertext fiction, interactive installations, VR, theme parks
- **Cognitive Process:** Physical/kinesthetic + cognitive participation, "user input"
- **Material Constraints:** Programmed possibilities, interface requirements, skill prerequisites
- **Immersion Type:** Physical presence in mediated environment (most intense)
- **Temporal Control:** Player-determined within programmed parameters
- **Strengths:** Agency, personalization, active co-creation, ludic pleasure
- **Limitations:** Transparency required (no metafiction), skill barriers, programmatic constraints

**Cross-Modal Relationships:**
```
TELLING ←→ SHOWING
├── Novel to film (most studied)
├── Film to novel (novelization)
├── Play to film
└── Multitrack vs unitrack media

SHOWING ←→ SHOWING
├── Film to opera
├── Stage to screen
├── Dance to film
└── Different performance media

INTERACTING ←→ TELLING/SHOWING
├── Novel to video game
├── Film to video game
├── Hyperfiction experiments
└── Transmedia franchises
```

#### **#TRANSCODING-THEORY-COMPLETE**

**Master Tag:** #Intersemiotic-Translation-Framework

**Definition:** "A shift from one sign system to another" - not merely medium change but fundamental reconception of how story is told

**Sub-Components:**
- #Remediation-Process: "Reformatting" inherent in cross-medium adaptation
- #Convention-Shift: Different media = different signifying systems and audience expectations
- #Gains-And-Losses-Principle: Every adaptation sacrifices something while gaining something
- #Creative-Reinterpretation: Not reproduction but active meaning-making

**Theoretical Lineage:**
- Walter Benjamin: Translation as engagement revealing new aspects of source
- Recent translation theory: Inter-cultural and inter-temporal communication
- NOT paraphrase or reproduction
- NOT fidelity-bound equivalence

---

#### **#FIDELITY-CRITICISM-DISMANTLED-COMPLETE**

**Master Tag:** #Anti-Fidelity-Paradigm

**Historical Problem:**
- Dominated adaptation studies for decades
- Based on false assumptions: adapters aim to "reproduce" adapted text
- Morally loaded discourse of "faithfulness"
- Hierarchical valorization of "original"

**Hutcheon's Counter-Arguments:**
1. **Repetition ≠ Replication:** Adaptation is "repetition without replication"
2. **Multiple Intentions:** Adapters may seek to erase, question, contest, OR honor source
3. **Oscillation Non-Hierarchical:** Movement between texts is lateral not vertical
4. **Anachronistic in Post-Theory Era:** Kristeva, Derrida, Foucault invalidate originality myths
5. **"Second" ≠ "Secondary":** Temporal posteriority ≠ inferiority

**Replacement Framework:**
- Judge adaptations on creativity, skill, autonomy
- Analyze as "deliberate, announced, extended revisitations"
- Examine adaptation-as-adaptation (double-vision)
- Consider success in new medium's terms

---

#### **#OSCILLATION-EXPERIENCE-COMPLETE**

**Master Tag:** #Palimpsestic-Reception-Theory

**Core Phenomenon:**
When audiences know adapted work, they experience "constant oscillation" between:
- Prior text (memory, expectations, interpretations)
- New adaptation (immediate experience, differences, innovations)

**Key Insights:**
1. **Direction-Agnostic:** Works whether film → novel or novel → film
2. **Memory-Dependent:** Requires audience knowledge of adapted work
3. **Pleasure Source:** Recognition AND surprise create aesthetic experience
4. **Interpretive Richness:** Multiple versions create "stereophony of echoes" (Barthes)

**Knowing vs Unknowing Audiences:**
- #Knowing-Audiences: Experience as adaptation (double awareness)
- #Unknowing-Audiences: Experience as autonomous work
- Both experiences valid but fundamentally different

### 2.2 Detailed Sub-Component Analysis

#### **#MEDIUM-SPECIFICITY-DEBATE-DECONSTRUCTED**

**Historical Context:**
- Lessing's *Laocöon* (1766): Painting = space, Poetry = time
- Greenberg's "Towards a Newer Laocöon" (1940): Modernist self-reflexivity
- Each art theorizes its specificity to gain cultural capital

**Hutcheon's Intervention:**
- Rejects essentialist specificity claims
- Proposes **modes of engagement** as more useful framework
- Acknowledges material constraints but emphasizes possibilities
- Focus: What can medium DO rather than what it IS

**Testing Theoretical Clichés:**

**Cliché #1:** "Only telling mode has flexibility for point of view"
- **Debunked:** Film uses camera positioning, editing, sound design for POV
- **Example:** *Adaptation* (Jonze) - multiple metafictional POVs

**Cliché #2:** "Only telling mode can render interiority"
- **Debunked:** Film uses montage, sound, mise-en-scène for interiority
- **Examples:** Expressionist cinema, voice-over, musical scoring

**Cliché #3:** "Film cannot handle past tense or temporal complexity"
- **Debunked:** Flashbacks, editing rhythms, sepia tones, etc.
- **Examples:** *Hiroshima Mon Amour*, memory films

**Cliché #4:** "Film cannot convey ambiguity, irony, metaphor"
- **Debunked:** Visual/aural symbolism, genre subversion, casting choices
- **Examples:** Cronenberg's metaphorical body horror

#### **#ADAPTERS-MOTIVATIONS-COMPLETE**

**Who Is the Adapter? (Multiplicity Problem)**
In collaborative media (film, theatre), adapter includes:
- Screenwriter/playwright
- Director
- Cinematographer
- Editor
- Sound designer
- Actors
- Producers
- All contribute to interpretive choices

**Why Adapt? (Complex Motivations)**

**#Economic-Lures:**
- Name recognition reduces risk
- "Tried and tested" (novels) or "tried and trusted" (classics)
- Franchise extension (sequels, prequels, spin-offs)
- Cross-platform monetization
- Best-seller → film reaches millions more

**#Legal-Constraints:**
- Copyright law (increasingly restrictive)
- Fair use complexities
- Fan culture conflicts with IP protection
- Corporate control vs. creative freedom

**#Cultural-Capital:**
- Prestige of adapting canonical works
- Educational/pedagogical value
- Cultural preservation (oral traditions → film)
- Reanimation of stories for new audiences

**#Personal-Political-Motives:**
- Update problematic politics (gender, race, colonialism)
- Contested homage (Oedipal relationship to source)
- Appropriation with critical commentary
- Salvaging endangered cultural heritage

**#Carmelite-Nuns-Case-Study:**
Extended analysis of multiple adaptations over 30 years:
- Gertrud von Le Fort's *Die Letzte am Schafott* (novella, 1931)
- Georges Bernanos' *Dialogues des Carmélites* (screenplay/novel, 1949)
- Poulenc's opera *Dialogues des Carmélites* (1957)
- Film versions
- Each adapter: different obsessions, politics, skills, contexts

---

#### **#AUDIENCE-PLEASURES-COMPLETE**

**#Repetition-With-Variation:**
"Comfort of ritual combined with piquancy of surprise"
- Recognition and remembrance
- Change and novelty
- Thematic/narrative persistence + material variation

**#Multiple-Versions-Experience:**
- No single "Ur-text" (no originary authority)
- Each version = interpretation
- Intertextual richness accumulates

**#Immersion-Types:**

**Telling Mode Immersion:**
- Imaginative world-building
- Deep character identification
- Conceptual work required

**Showing Mode Immersion:**
- Perceptual intensity
- Psychoanalytic identification/projection
- Collective experience (theatre/cinema)

**Interacting Mode Immersion:**
- Physical/kinesthetic presence
- Agency and control
- Skill-based mastery pleasure
- "On verge of mastery and losing control"

**#Fan-Culture-Pleasures:**
- Participatory creation
- Community building
- Ownership and personalization
- Remix and reinterpretation
- Digital accessibility enables mass adaptation

---

#### **#CONTEXT-VASTNESS-COMPLETE**

**#Transcultural-Adaptation:**
When stories "travel" across:
- Geographic boundaries
- Linguistic differences
- Cultural value systems
- Historical periods

**Result:** "Change is inevitable even without conscious updating"
- Political valence shifts
- Meaning transforms
- Power structures revealed/challenged

**#Indigenization-Process:**
- Stories adapted to local cultural contexts
- Stereotypes confronted and subverted
- Dominant narratives challenged
- Colonial legacies interrogated

**#Carmen-Analysis-Extended:**
Multiple adaptations of Mérimée's novella (1845) → Bizet's opera (1875):
- African versions (challenge exoticization)
- Carmen Jones (1943/1954) - African American transposition
- MTV's Carmen: A Hip-Hopera (2001)
- Each: challenges racial/gendered stereotypes of "gypsy" femme fatale
- Demonstrates how context radically alters meaning

## PHASE 3: METHODOLOGICAL DEEP ANALYSIS

### 3.1 Process Extraction: Hutcheon's Analytical Method

**Sequential Steps for Analyzing Adaptation:**

**Step 1: Identify Mode of Engagement**
- Determine if shift involves telling, showing, interacting
- Note if shift crosses modes or stays within single mode
- Consider implications of modal affordances/constraints

**Step 2: Analyze Transcoding Strategies**
- What conventions change?
- What sign systems are involved?
- How is meaning recoded?
- What is gained/lost?

**Step 3: Examine Adapter Intentions**
- Economic motivations?
- Legal constraints?
- Cultural capital goals?
- Personal/political agendas?
- Tribute, critique, or appropriation?

**Step 4: Consider Audience Experience**
- Knowing or unknowing audience?
- What oscillation occurs?
- How does immersion differ?
- What pleasures are enabled?

**Step 5: Contextualize Culturally/Historically**
- When/where was adapted text created?
- When/where is adaptation received?
- What travels across contexts?
- How does meaning shift?

**Step 6: Evaluate as Adaptation AND Autonomous Work**
- Success in new medium's terms?
- Creative interpretation demonstrated?
- Autonomous artistic achievement?
- Relationship to adapted text enriching?

**Decision Points:**
- If unknown audience: treat as autonomous work
- If known audience: double-vision analysis
- If multiple adaptations exist: trace evolution/variation
- If transcultural: examine indigenization process

**Quality Indicators:**
- Creativity and skill in transcoding
- Autonomous aesthetic value
- Productive relationship to source(s)
- Success in new medium/mode
- Cultural/political awareness

## PHASE 4: CRITICAL EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

### 4.1 Theoretical Analysis

**Underlying Assumptions:**
1. **Anti-Essentialism:** No medium has inherent essence; all have material constraints and creative possibilities
2. **Experiential Priority:** Human engagement more important than formal properties alone
3. **Cultural Embeddedness:** All art exists in social/historical/economic contexts
4. **Interpretive Plurality:** Multiple valid readings/adaptations possible
5. **Agency Valorization:** Creators and audiences are active meaning-makers

**Paradigmatic Position:**
- **Post-Structuralist:** Intertextuality, anti-originality, death of author
- **Post-Fidelity:** Rejects normative source-oriented criticism
- **Phenomenological:** Emphasizes lived experience of engagement
- **Cultural Materialist:** Attends to economic/political dimensions
- **Anti-Colonial:** Challenges Western/white/masculine defaults

**Epistemological Framework:**
- Knowledge derived from practice (inductive theory-building)
- Multiple examples > single case studies
- Commonalities + differences = richer understanding
- Theory emerges from patterns across instances
- Provisional, pragmatic rather than totalizing

**Ideological Implications:**
- **Democratizing:** All adaptations have potential value
- **Anti-Capitalist Elements:** Critiques commodification while acknowledging economic realities
- **Progressive Politics:** Updates problematic representations welcomed
- **Fan Empowerment:** Validates participatory culture (especially in Epilogue)

### 7.2 Quick Reference Guide

**ESSENTIAL CONCEPTS AT A GLANCE:**

**What is Adaptation?**
- Product: Announced, extensive, specific transcoding
- Process: (Re)interpretation + (re)creation
- Reception: Intertextual palimpsestic experience

**Three Modes of Engagement:**
- **Telling:** Novels, stories → imaginative immersion
- **Showing:** Film, theatre, opera → perceptual immersion
- **Interacting:** Games, VR → physical/kinesthetic immersion

**Key Arguments:**
1. Fidelity criticism inadequate—focus on creativity instead
2. "Second" does not mean "secondary" or inferior
3. Adaptation is repetition without replication
4. All media have constraints AND possibilities
5. Context changes meaning even without updates
6. Fan adaptation is legitimate creative practice

**How to Analyze Adaptations:**
1. Identify modes of engagement involved
2. Examine transcoding strategies
3. Consider adapter motivations
4. Analyze audience experience (knowing/unknowing)
5. Contextualize culturally and historically
6. Evaluate as both adaptation AND autonomous work

**Common Misconceptions Debunked:**
- Only literature can do interiority → FALSE (film uses montage, sound, etc.)
- Only film can show action → FALSE (literature has techniques too)
- Games are inferior to literature → FALSE (different pleasures, immersions)
- Adaptations are always inferior → FALSE (judging by wrong criteria)
- Fidelity determines quality → FALSE (creativity and skill matter)
