EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Document Type: Pedagogical treatise / creative-psychology manual
Disciplinary Domain: Theatre pedagogy | Performance studies | Psychology of creativity

Core Thesis:
Impro proposes that creativity and spontaneous intelligence are innate human capacities systematically suppressed by formal education and social conditioning. Through structured improvisation exercises, performers can relearn the natural responsiveness of childhood, unlocking imagination, storytelling, and authentic presence.

Structural Division: Four Parts
	1.	Status – Social interaction and improvised power dynamics
	2.	Spontaneity – Blocks to creativity and techniques for freedom
	3.	Narrative Skills – Structure and intuition in story creation
	4.	Masks and Trance – Embodiment, ritual, and altered states

Methodology: Phenomenological and empirical — Johnstone derives theory from studio practice and student observation rather than academic abstraction.

Distinctive Contribution:
Introduces a psychological-performative model of creativity later absorbed into actor training, education, and organizational innovation. The concept of “status transactions” became a cornerstone in modern impro and communication studies.

Pedagogical Logic:
Each chapter presents a problem (block to spontaneity) → diagnosis → exercise → reflection.
Johnstone’s voice oscillates between teacher, researcher, and storyteller.

Intended Audience: Actors, teachers, directors, writers, and anyone concerned with unblocking creativity.

2.7 #CREATIVE-BLOCKS AND UNLEARNING

Definition: A creative “block” is an internalized authority structure—voices of evaluation that suppress instinct.
Pedagogical Principle: Unlearning is as deliberate as learning: dismantle obedience, recover curiosity.

Cause of Block	Johnstone’s Diagnosis	Counter-Technique
Fear of failure	Conditioned by grading systems	Games that reward mistakes
Desire to control outcome	Ego interference	Speed drills; immediate response
Need to appear clever	Social status anxiety	Ban “cleverness”; praise obvious offers
Over-intellectualization	School conditioning	Physical games; word-association
Self-censorship	Fear of ridicule	Collective clowning; laughter rituals

Cross-Tags: #deconditioning #educational-critique #psychological-freedom #flow-state

⸻

2.8 #CHILD-MIND AS CREATIVE IDEAL

Concept: Children improvise effortlessly because they lack fear of error and hierarchy of taste.
Objective: Re-enter pre-critical play while retaining adult skill.
Analogy: The actor is a “trained child.”

Techniques:
	•	Spontaneous storytelling with no moral filter.
	•	“Yes, and …” acceptance to maintain flow.
	•	Imitation games to short-circuit originality anxiety.

Cross-Tags: #play-theory #innocence-as-method #creativity-psychology #flow-continuum

2.11 #STATUS-WORK AS SOCIAL-MICROSCOPY

Johnstone treats status play as a diagnostic instrument revealing hidden hierarchies.
	•	Up-status: assertion, confidence, blocking offers.
	•	Down-status: submission, invitation, yielding.
	•	Dynamic exchange: true drama = oscillation.

Key Exercise: Converse while maintaining predetermined status; observe energy shifts.

Analytical Outcome:
Students discover that “character” is not psychological depth but status behaviour across context—a performance grammar transferable to film blocking or dialogue rhythm.

Cross-Tags: #social-semiotics #performance-psychology #character-construction #embodied-analysis
