## DRAMA & SCREENWRITING FUNDAMENTALS

### **PROFILE 1: Story - Robert McKee**

**Publication**: 1997 | **Domain**: Dramatic Theory, Scene Craft, Story Design

#### Unique Contributions
- Most philosophically rigorous approach to screenwriting
- Scene as fundamental unit of story construction
- Story values and their transformation as core of drama
- Controlling Idea as story's ultimate meaning
- Subtext as essential dialogue principle
- Deep exploration of genre worldviews
- Classical design vs. other story paradigms
- Gap between expectation and result as dramatic engine

#### Methodological Approach
- **Philosophy**: Aristotelian dramatic principles applied to modern cinema
- **Teaching Style**: Socratic questioning, deep analysis
- **Organization**: Conceptual and theoretical rather than step-by-step
- **Emphasis**: Understanding WHY before learning HOW
- **Target**: Serious writers seeking deep craft mastery

#### Strengths
- Unmatched philosophical depth
- Explains fundamental dramatic principles
- Applicable across all genres and formats
- Develops critical thinking about story
- Provides language for discussing craft
- Timeless principles, not trends

#### Limitations
- Dense, requires multiple readings
- Less practical step-by-step guidance
- Can feel abstract for beginners
- Minimal comedy-specific content
- Requires significant study investment

#### Integration Points
- **Pairs Well With**: Truby (architecture), Seger (problem-solving), Field (structure)
- **Complements**: Any practical methodology (provides philosophical foundation)
- **Conflicts With**: Formulaic approaches (Snyder) without nuanced understanding
- **Best Combined With**: Seger for McKee philosophy + practical application

#### Strategic Usage
- **When to Use**:
  - Developing deep understanding of dramatic principles
  - Solving complex story problems
  - Understanding genre conventions
  - Deepening scene craft
  - Exploring story meaning and theme
  
- **When Not to Use**:
  - Need quick practical guidance
  - Writing pure comedy (limited comedy content)
  - Absolute beginners (too advanced)
  - Looking for formula or template

- **Optimal Learning Stage**: Intermediate to advanced (after mastering basics)

- **Time Investment**: 50-100 hours for full mastery

#### Quick Reference
- **Best For**: Scene craft, subtext, dramatic theory, controlling idea
- **Avoid For**: Step-by-step structure, comedy mechanics, beginner templates
- **Page Count**: 466 pages
- **Density**: Very high - requires focused study
- **Re-read Value**: Extremely high - reveals new insights with each reading

- Revelation sequence structure
- Character-theme integration system

#### Methodological Approach
- **Philosophy**: Organic architecture from character and theme
- **Teaching Style**: Systematic, comprehensive, architectural
- **Organization**: Linear progression through story development
- **Emphasis**: Planning and design before writing
- **Target**: Writers seeking complete systematic methodology

#### Strengths
- Most comprehensive single methodology
- Character and theme deeply integrated
- Sophisticated understanding of story architecture
- Works across genres
- Provides complete story design system
- Addresses WHY and HOW simultaneously

#### Limitations

- Comedy less developed than drama

#### Strategic Usage

- Specific page number guidelines (25-27, 85-90)
- Dramatic Need as character driver
- Context/Content structural framework
- Setup/Confrontation/Resolution act structure
- Accessible three-act teaching methodology
- Democratized screenwriting instruction

#### Methodological Approach
- **Philosophy**: Structure as universal framework
- **Teaching Style**: Clear, accessible, directive
- **Organization**: Sequential, building from basics
- **Emphasis**: Practical application, clear examples
- **Target**: Beginning to intermediate writers

#### Strengths
- Extremely clear and accessible
- Excellent teaching tool
- Provides concrete guidelines
- Easy to understand and apply
- Good entry point for beginners
- Applicable to most commercial films

#### Limitations
- Can feel rigid or formulaic
- Less philosophical depth than McKee/Truby

- Limited character depth discussion
- Comedy less developed

#### Integration Points
- **Pairs Well With**: McKee (depth), Snyder (specificity), Seger (refinement)
- **Complements**: Any deep craft approach (provides structural foundation)
- **Conflicts With**: Flexible/organic approaches if applied too rigidly
- **Best Combined With**: McKee for Field structure + McKee scene craft

#### Strategic Usage
- **When to Use**:
  - Learning screenplay structure basics
  - Teaching beginners
  - Planning commercial feature structure
  - Analyzing film structure
  - Quick structural troubleshooting

- **When Not to Use**:
  - Experimental or non-traditional structures

#### Quick Reference
