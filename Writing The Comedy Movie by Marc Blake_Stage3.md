§1 COMEDY AS NARRATIVE PHYSICS (#narrative-physics)

1.1 Energy and Entropy in the Comic Universe (#energy-systems)

Marc Blake’s craft model treats every comic scene as an energetic exchange.
Each beat generates potential energy (setup) and releases kinetic energy (payoff).
The writer’s task: maintain entropy in balance—enough disorder to amuse, enough structure to sustain story logic.

Within PK paradigm:

Story = Thermodynamic System of Meaning.

Concept	Dramatic Equivalent	Comic Transformation
Potential Energy	Dramatic tension build-up	Expectation before joke
Kinetic Energy	Climactic release	Laughter / Recognition
Equilibrium	Resolution	Post-joke reset
Entropy	Conflict / confusion	Escalating chaos → comic order

A joke is a micro-explosion of restored order: tension converts into insight.
Thus comedy’s physics mirror Field’s cause-and-effect structure, refracted through timing.

⸻

1.2 Timing as Gravitational Constant (#timing)

Comedy obeys gravitational precision.
Remove one frame too soon → setup unreadable.
Hold one frame too long → energy decays.

Rule: Timing = Distance between Knowledge and Awareness.

Level	Structural Unit	Ideal Gap for Maximum Impact
Beat	Dialogue exchange	1 – 1.5 seconds
Scene	Physical action reaction	3 – 7 seconds
Sequence	Running gag callback	8 – 12 minutes
Act	Comic turning point	25 – 35 pages

Each gap calibrates cognitive lag: the audience completes the pattern milliseconds after recognition—triggering laughter.

Timing thus becomes a moral metric: it measures empathy through pacing.
A fast joke invites intellect; a slow burn invites compassion.

⸻

1.3 Force Vectors — Setup, Conflict, Payoff (#force-vectors)

Blake frames the joke as a three-vector system:
	1.	Setup Vector (+x): Information flow → Expectation.
	2.	Conflict Vector (–y): Contradiction or obstacle → Incongruity.
	3.	Resolution Vector (+z): Twist → Cognitive closure.

Equation:

COMIC FORCE = (Setup × Conflict) → Payoff / Audience Latency

Latency = milliseconds between perception and understanding.
Short latency → snappy wit; long latency → slow reveal.

⸻

1.4 Rhythmic Topology (#rhythm-and-structure)

Every comic story oscillates on a sinusoidal curve of tension and release.
Blake calls this the “Laughter Wave.”

Phase	Description	PK Analogue
A – Setup Zone	Exposition camouflaged as confusion	#setup-phase
B – Destabilization	Stakes emerge through error	#inciting-incident
C – Escalation Loop	Compounded misunderstanding	#progressive-complication
D – Climactic Collapse	Total chaos / truth revealed	#climax
E – Reset / Aftermath	New equilibrium with self-awareness	#denouement

Unlike drama’s smooth curve, comedy’s oscillation is rapid and frequent—mini climaxes embedded every two to three minutes.

The writer engineers a beat frequency that keeps audience engaged without emotional fatigue.

⸻

1.5 Polarity Switches and Value Dynamics (#value-shifts)

Blake inherits McKee’s concept of value change and applies it at micro level.
Every laugh marks a flip in value polarity.

Value Axis	+ State	– State	Comic Trigger
Dignity	Poise	Humiliation	Slapstick / social exposure
Knowledge	Awareness	Ignorance	Verbal irony
Love	Connection	Isolation	Miscommunication
Power	Control	Chaos	Situational farce
Morality	Integrity	Hypocrisy	Satire

Cumulative flips form the story’s emotional waveform.
Measured density ≈ one flip per half-page maintains rhythmic momentum.

⸻

1.6 Character as Energy Conductor (#character-function)

Characters are circuit components in the comedy engine.
Each conducts or resists narrative current.

Role	Function	Symbolic Charge
Protagonist	Generates conflict through flawed logic	Positive cathode (+E)
Antagonist	Applies counter-logic / pressure	Negative anode (–E)
Sidekick	Regulates voltage via commentary	Ground (0)
Audience	Receives discharge as laughter	External circuit

Failure = short circuit (moment of truth).
Success = energy re-stabilized (new order).

Thus comedy’s “moral lesson” is electrical: too much ego current blows the fuse.

⸻

1.7 Cognitive Geometry (#spatial-logic)

The comic frame operates like visual geometry.
Spatial relationships create logic paths:
	•	Triangular composition = conflict loop (hero ↔ antagonist ↔ observer).
	•	Closed circles = repetition / routine.
	•	Broken symmetry = surprise / reversal.

Cinematographically, this translates to:
Setup → Asymmetry → Framing Reversal.
Each camera move becomes a punchline in visual grammar (#visual-irony).

⸻

1.8 Emotion as Velocity (#emotional-kinetics)

Comedy runs on emotional speed, not magnitude.
While drama lingers on feeling, comedy accelerates through it.

Formula:

Emotional Velocity = Δ Emotion / Δ Time

A rapid swing from confidence to panic to relief yields comic momentum.
Slower oscillation produces pathos (#dramedy boundary).

Writers modulate velocity via:
	1.	Sentence length (linguistic tempo).
	2.	Shot duration (filmic tempo).
	3.	Actor pause management (performance tempo).

⸻

1.9 Resonance and Echo (#callbacks)

Humor sustains through resonant memory.
A callback re-activates earlier data with variance ≈ 20–30 %.

Recognition + Difference = Pleasure × 2.

Within PK analytics, callback = “micro-adaptation” (Hutcheon’s repetition with variation).
Each echo strengthens system coherence (#structural-symmetry).

⸻

1.10 Noise Management (#information-theory)

Comedic clarity depends on signal-to-noise ratio.
Over-information kills surprise; under-information kills logic.

Blake’s diagnostic rule: 80 % setup comprehension / 20 % ambiguity triggers optimal laughter.
This mirrors Shannon’s information entropy curve applied to narrative.

⸻

1.11 From Gag to Story Engine (#scaling-law)

Blake scales the joke mechanism to full narrative length:

Scale	Structural Analog	Key Principle

Act	Chain of scenes	Reversal pattern repeats macro
Film	Three Acts + Callback Finale	Closure as meta-joke

Therefore, every successful comedy is a fractal of its own gags (#self-similarity).

⸻

1.12 Transferring Momentum Across Acts (#continuity-physics)

Transitions between acts must conserve momentum.
Blake employs bridging gags — comic devices that carry energy forward while shifting context.

Example pattern (schema):
	•	End Act I → Hero vows to change (ironically misguided).
	•	Bridge gag illustrates first attempt at change failing spectacularly.
	•	Audience energy transfers without pause.

This maintains narrative velocity ≈ constant (#continuity-law).

1.13 Feedback and Homeostasis (#adaptive-feedback)

In performance, audience laughter acts as real-time data.
Stage 3 interprets this as cybernetic feedback:

Input:  Joke → Output: Laughter → System Adjustment

Rewrites use recorded timing gaps to fine-tune beat length.
Comedy thus becomes a self-correcting organism (#creative-systems).

§2 CHARACTER ARCHITECTURE OF HUMOR (#character-architecture)

2.1 The Comic Protagonist as System Error (#hero-as-bug)

Marc Blake defines the comic hero as a glitch in the moral software.
Where dramatic heroes face external crisis, comic heroes generate it.
They are self-defeating algorithms running faulty code: pride, denial, obsession, vanity.

Principle: Comedy begins when a human flaw becomes procedural.

In PK terms, the protagonist is a recurring loop seeking resolution but feeding back on itself.
The story ends only when self-awareness breaks recursion.

⸻

2.2 Truby Integration — Moral Argument Through Flaw (#truby-link)

Truby’s moral-systemic theory aligns precisely: each comic flaw represents a warped value algorithm.

Flaw Code	Manifestation	Moral Lesson
EgoOverflow()	Arrogance → Public Humiliation	Humility restores balance
LoveNull()	Emotional incompetence → Loss → Growth	Empathy learned through failure
ControlLoop()	Micromanagement → Chaos	Letting go as grace
TruthError()	Self-deception → Exposure	Authenticity rewarded
FaithDivide()	Cynicism → Wonder rediscovered	Belief as renewal

The comic narrative is therefore a debugging story—repairing code through ridicule.

⸻

2.3 The Hero’s Journey in Comic Form (#comic-journey)

Blake reshapes Campbell’s monomyth into the Comic Cycle:
	1.	Flawed Ordinary World — egoic equilibrium.
	2.	False Call — mistaken belief that success is easy.
	3.	Threshold of Humiliation — first public failure.
	4.	Descent into Absurdity — chain reaction of errors.
	5.	Moment of Void — all schemes collapse.
	6.	Comic Revelation — truth arrives through laughter.
	7.	Return with Self-Knowledge — partial enlightenment, new equilibrium.

Unlike tragedy’s irreversible downfall, comedy resets the system; awareness > annihilation.

§3 STRUCTURAL TEMPLATES & COMIC PLOT DESIGN (#structural-templates)

3.1 Structure as the Invisible Joke (#structure-as-joke)

In Marc Blake’s system, structure is the ultimate punchline.
Every act break is a joke whose setup began hundreds of pages earlier.
To the untrained eye, comedy appears spontaneous; in practice, it is an engineered chain of reversals.
Stage 3 identifies the plot spine as a predictable pattern of unpredictability.

Formula: Structure = Setup / Payoff × Irony.

Comedy thus inherits Field’s three-act logic but transforms each beat into a repetition with contradiction.

⸻

3.2 The Comic Three-Act Model (#comic-three-act)

Act	Function	Dominant Energy	Key Question	PK Analogue
Act I – Chaos in Order	Establish flawed equilibrium	Curiosity	“Why doesn’t this person see?”	#setup
Act II – Order in Chaos	Escalating misperceptions	Frustration	“How far can this go?”	#progressive-complication
Act III – Harmony in Truth	Collision of self with consequence	Catharsis	“What has been learned?”	#resolution

Tempo ratio ≈ 1:1.5:1.
Midpoint functions as inverse revelation — protagonist misreads event as success, propelling Act II into deeper error.

⸻

3.3 The Rule of Three (#rule-of-three)

The Rule of Three governs both micro (gags) and macro (plot).
	1.	Establish pattern.
	2.	Reinforce pattern.
	3.	Break pattern.

This rhythmic law aligns with audience neural expectation; the third beat reconfigures memory of the first two.

In structural terms:
	•	Act I = Setup
	•	Act II = Variation
	•	Act III = Subversion

Every plotline must close with a third-beat inversion (#pattern-completion).

3.4 Reversal Loops (#reversal-loops)

A reversal loop occurs when intention and outcome invert simultaneously.
In comedy, every escalation cycle should culminate in such a loop.

Loop Type	Description	Structural Placement

Relational Loop	Characters exchange roles	Act II midpoint
Ideological Loop	Belief exposed as folly	Act III crisis
Cosmic Loop	World conspires ironically	Climax

Each loop refreshes entropy and resets audience engagement.

3.5 Set-Piece Engineering (#setpieces)

Blake approaches set-pieces as macro-gags: visual and narrative spectacles engineered from contradiction.

Blueprint:
	1.	Premise — a confined arena or premise of escalating tension.

	4.	Explosion — comic payoff aligning all prior beats.

Each set-piece must advance plot and amplify theme.

Act	Confusion per Scene	Audience Emotion

III	5+ concurrent misreads	Catharsis + Relief

This curve maintains suspense without threat of tragedy — the engine of safe chaos.

3.7 The Midpoint as Comic Inversion (#midpoint)

At midpoint, the protagonist believes they are winning.
This false summit becomes the hinge of irony: the success is merely a higher platform from which to fall.

McKee’s “Value Flip” becomes here a double-negative reveal — knowledge appears to rise but collapses under contradiction.
Every strong comedy pivots on this delusional triumph.

§4 GENRE INTERACTIONS & COMIC SUB-SYSTEMS (#genre-interactions)

⸻

4.1 Comedy as Meta-Genre (#meta-genre)

Blake views comedy as a meta-genre—a lens that refracts every narrative mode into irony.
Rather than existing beside drama, horror, or romance, it infects them, converting sincerity into tension between expectation and absurdity.
Where other genres promise closure, comedy promises exposure: it unmasks the fragile logic of the genre itself.

“Every genre has rules; comedy’s rule is to reveal the rule.”

In PK terms, comedy acts as an auditing protocol inside the storytelling operating system.

⸻

4.2 Rom-Com Architecture (#romcom)

Element	Function	Structural Ratio	Emotional Polarity
Meet Cute	Collision of incompatible logics	5 %	Surprise / Charm
Miscommunication Spiral	Escalating misunderstanding	40 %	Delight ↔ Frustration
Moment of Truth	Emotional exposure	15 %	Humiliation → Empathy
Reunion	Re-synchronization of values	10 %	Joy → Closure

Blake adapts Truby’s desire vs need model: love comedies dramatize emotional illiteracy corrected through failure.
Timing equals sincerity divided by ego.

⸻

4.3 Satire & Political Comedy (#satire)

Satire operates as the ideological branch of comedy: revelation through exaggeration.
Blake identifies a double-entry ledger of power:

Mechanism	Target	Energy Source
Parody	Cultural form	Recognition + variation
Irony	Ideology	Displacement of authority
Absurdism	System itself	Logical implosion

Satire’s strength lies in proportionality—too gentle, and it flatters; too cruel, and it alienates.
Ethical calibration aligns with Hutcheon’s oscillation: empathy ↔ critique.

⸻

4.4 Farce as Kinetic System (#farce)

Farce is pure thermodynamics—characters trapped in acceleration loops.
Spatial compression + temporal pressure = laughter.

Key parameters:
	•	Confined Arena (office, hotel, family home)
	•	Clock Mechanism (urgency driver)
	•	Identity Confusion Chain (roles swap every five pages)
	•	Physical Feedback (collision per minute ratio ≥ 1)

Outcome = entropy without despair.
Blake calls farce the “laboratory of cause and effect.”

⸻

4.5 Dark Comedy (#darkcomedy)

Dark comedy converts tragedy into reframed empathy.
When pain becomes observation, distance creates wit.

Variable	Optimal Zone	Hazard
Moral Distance	1–2 steps from suffering	Too close = cruelty
Tone Contrast	30 % light within dark	Collapse = nihilism
Redemption Signal	Late recognition beat	Absent = bitterness

Blake fuses McKee’s value flip with emotional risk management: laughter at the edge of fear.

⸻

4.6 Parody & Meta-Comedy (#parody)

Parody is adaptation as commentary, echoing Hutcheon’s palimpsestic model: recognition plus distortion.
Each borrowed trope functions as setup; deviation provides punchline.

PK Mapping:

Source Recognition → Oscillation → Transformation.

Sustainable parody demands affection for its subject; contempt erases context.
Thus, Blake’s guideline: mock with knowledge.

⸻

4.7 Horror-Comedy (#horror-comedy)

In hybrid form, horror’s tension curve merges with comedy’s release curve.
Equation:

Fear + Relief = Laughter of Survival

Both depend on timing lag between threat and reaction.
Comedy punctures dread by restoring agency; horror intensifies comedy’s catharsis.

⸻

4.8 Crime and Heist Comedies (#crime)

Blake interprets crime comedy as order versus incompetence.
Capers rely on precision until human error derails perfection.
Every plan equals a setup; failure equals punchline.
Truby’s “reversal of desire” — criminal wants money but needs morality — drives closure.

Diagnostic metric: one botched plan per act.

⸻

4.9 Workplace & Institutional Comedy (#institutional)

The system is the antagonist.
Workplace sitcoms model bureaucratic loop logic: process without purpose.
Each episode = iteration of attempted progress reset to zero.
Narrative energy arises from hope colliding with procedure.

Pedagogical note: mapping status transactions per scene reveals where humor lives.

⸻

4.10 Road Comedy (#road)

Movement = metaphor for self-correction.
The road plot is a spatial farce: geography mirrors growth.

Stage	Physical Shift	Emotional Shift
Departure	Escape from control	Illusion of freedom
Mid-Route Chaos	Confined mobility	Recognition of flaw
Arrival	Return with new humility	Re-integration

Road comedies illustrate entropy management over time: motion without progress → insight through repetition.

⸻

4.11 Fantasy & Sci-Fi Comedy (#speculative)

Speculative worlds permit logical experimentation: “What if the laws of reality were funny?”
Blake treats genre physics as metaphor for human logic errors.
The further from reality, the clearer the commentary.

Calibration rule: 1 impossible element per 10 realistic ones.
This maintains audience anchoring (#suspension-of-disbelief).

⸻

4.12 Social & Cultural Comedy (#culture)

Cultural comedy translates social codes into behavioral puzzles.
Blake advocates specificity over universality: authentic detail becomes gateway to recognition.

Framework:
	1.	Insider View — Truth within group.
	2.	Outsider Observation — Comparative perspective.
	3.	Synthesis — Empathy without erasure.

Ethical guardrail: punch lines must punch upward.

⸻

4.13 Musical Comedy (#musical)

Song functions as rhythmic exaggeration of dialogue.
When emotion exceeds speech, it sings.
Blake maps music beats to comic beats:

Beat Type	Narrative Analogue	Frequency

Chorus	Punchline reinforcement	recurring
Bridge	Structural turn point	midpoint
Finale Reprise	Closure callback	climax

Integration metric: tempo alignment ± 5 % between music and joke waveform.

⸻

4.14 Animation & Hyper-Real Comedy (#animation)

Animation liberates physics but demands tighter logic.
Exaggeration multiplies risk of noise; clarity = king.
Each frame is a beat — visual density replaces dialogue density.

Rule: One conceptual gag per shot max.
Over-stacking visuals produces cognitive overload.

4.15 Improvisational Comedy (#improv)

Improvisation is process, not genre.
Its governing algorithm is “Yes, and …” — acceptance before addition.
Blake links this to PK’s adaptive feedback: every offer = data input; scene evolves through collaborative processing.

Improv teaches writers to model resilience within constraint.

§6 ADAPTIVE FEEDBACK & EVOLUTION FROM PAGE TO SCREEN (#adaptive-feedback)

⸻

6.1 Comedy as a Living System (#living-system)

In Blake’s model the finished screenplay is not an endpoint but a feedback organism.
Each production stage—read-through, rehearsal, shoot, edit, and release—returns data that refines the writer’s internal algorithm.
A joke’s survival rate across these phases becomes a measure of narrative resilience.

“Comedy dies where feedback stops.”

Stage 3 defines this feedback loop as Adaptive Comedy System (ACS): a continuous circulation between creation → execution → observation → revision.

⸻

6.2 Table Read as Prototype (#prototype)

The table read acts as the first live simulation of timing physics.
Writers document:

Variable	Metric	Ideal Range
Laugh Latency	Time between line and response	0.4 – 1.5 s
Overlap Rate	% of dialogue lost to laughter	≤ 10 %
Energy Decay	Drop in audience engagement per scene	< 15 %
Callback Recognition	Audience memory hit	≥ 70 %

These readings convert instinct into data. High latency → rewrite for clarity; low latency → expand tension.

⸻

6.3 Rehearsal Feedback (#rehearsal-loop)

Actors become human sensors of rhythm.
Each run reveals micro-adjustments: breath length, gesture timing, spatial rhythm.
Blake treats directors as signal amplifiers—they regulate volume and pacing without altering content integrity.
Resulting timing tables feed into the script’s next iteration.

⸻

6.4 On-Set Adaptation (#onset)

Shooting reveals new comedic variables: camera angle = timing vector; lens length = comic proximity.
Principles:
	1.	Wide Frame = objectivity → laughter through pattern.
	2.	Close-Up = empathy → laughter through recognition.
	3.	Cut Timing = punchline delivery. Frame hold ≤ 0.3 s after reaction peak.

Writers attending set document real-time discrepancies to improve later drafts (#production-feedback).

⸻
