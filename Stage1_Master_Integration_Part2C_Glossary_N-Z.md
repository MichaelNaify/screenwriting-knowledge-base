## OBSTACLE

**Universal Definition**: Barriers, challenges, or opposition that prevent the protagonist from achieving their goal, creating conflict and forcing difficult choices.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Truby (Anatomy), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Obstacles force revealing choices
- Field: Progressive obstacles build to crisis
- Truby: Opposition as essential story element
- Snyder: "Bad Guys Close In" - obstacles intensify

**Tags**: #structure #conflict #obstacle #opposition #complication #mckee #field #truby

---

## PARADIGM

**Universal Definition** (Field): The three-act structural model Field uses as a teaching and organizational tool for screenplay construction, with plot points at pages 25-27 and 85-90.

**Source Coverage**: Field (Screenplay)

**Key Variations**:
- Field's signature concept
- Simple visualization tool
- Framework for organizing story

**Tags**: #structure #paradigm #three_act #field #structure_foundation

---

## PLOT POINT

**Universal Definition** (Field): An event that "hooks" the action and spins it in another direction, occurring at the end of Act One and Act Two, propelling the story forward.

**Source Coverage**: Field (Screenplay), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat - as "Break Into Two/Three")

**Key Variations**:
- Field: Plot Point One (page 25-27), Plot Point Two (page 85-90)
- Snyder: "Break Into Two" and "Break Into Three"
- Seger: Turning points in structure

**Tags**: #structure #plot_point #turning_point #field #snyder #seger

---

## PROTAGONIST

**Universal Definition**: The central character whose desire drives the story, who faces the most significant obstacles, makes the most important choices, and experiences the primary character arc.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Truby (Anatomy), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat), Owen (Adaptation)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Character with conscious and unconscious desires
- Field: Character with dramatic need
- Truby: Character with desire line
- Snyder: Hero in hero's journey
- Seger: Central character with clear goal

**Tags**: #character #character_foundation #protagonist #hero #mckee #field #truby #snyder #seger

---

## REWRITING

**Universal Definition**: The process of systematically revising a screenplay to solve problems, strengthen elements, incorporate feedback, and elevate the script to professional standards.

**Source Coverage**: Chitlik (Rewrite), Seger (Making Great), Field (Screenplay), McKee (Story)

**Key Variations**:
- Chitlik: Most comprehensive rewriting methodology
- Seger: Problem-diagnosis and solution-focused
- Field: Structural revision emphasis
- McKee: Scene-level refinement

**Tags**: #rewriting #revision #development #chitlik #seger #field #professional

---

## RUNNER

**Universal Definition** (Finn): A recurring joke, gag, or bit that appears multiple times throughout a sitcom episode or series, building through repetition and variation.

**Source Coverage**: Finn (Sitcom), Vorhaus (Comic Toolbox - as "running gag"), Blake (Comedy Movie), Sacks (interviews)

**Key Variations**:
- Finn: Television-specific runner technique
- Vorhaus: Running gag in comedy structure
- Blake: Recurring comedy elements

**Tags**: #comedy_structure #runner #running_gag #callback #repetition #finn #vorhaus

---

## SAVE THE CAT

**Universal Definition** (Snyder): A moment early in the screenplay where the protagonist does something likeable, sympathetic, or heroic that makes the audience "like" them and want to follow their journey.

**Source Coverage**: Snyder (Save the Cat)

**Key Variations**:
- Snyder's signature concept
- Creates character sympathy early
- Can be literal (save a cat) or metaphorical

**Tags**: #character #character_development #save_the_cat #sympathy #likability #snyder

---

## SCENE

**Universal Definition**: The fundamental building block of screenplay structure - a unit of action occurring in continuous time and space that changes the value-charge of a character's situation.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story - most comprehensive), Field (Screenplay), Seger (Making Great), Truby (Anatomy), Snyder (Save the Cat)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Scene as fundamental unit; beats within scenes
- Field: Scene as action unit
- Seger: Scene structure and problems
- Truby: Scene in character journey context

**Tags**: #structure #scene #scene_structure #beat #mckee #field #seger #truby

---

## STATUS

**Universal Definition** (Johnstone): The relative dominance or submission between characters in any given moment, expressed through behavior, body language, and dialogue, creating comedy and dramatic tension through status shifts and plays.

**Source Coverage**: Johnstone (Impro), Vorhaus (Comic Toolbox), Close (Truth in Comedy)

**Key Variations**:
- Johnstone: Most comprehensive status theory
- Vorhaus: Status in comedy construction
- Close: Status in improv patterns

**Tags**: #comedy_improv #improvisation #status #johnstone #vorhaus #character_relationships

---

## STRUCTURE

**Universal Definition**: The selection and arrangement of story events (scenes, sequences, acts) in a specific order to create maximum dramatic or comedic impact and audience engagement.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay), Truby (Anatomy), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat), Owen (Adaptation), Blake (Comedy)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Story design; organic structure
- Field: Paradigm; three-act template
- Truby: 22 building blocks; character-driven
- Snyder: Beat sheet; 15-beat formula
- Seger: Flexible problem-solving approach
- Blake: Comedy-specific structure

**Tags**: #structure #structure_foundation #story_design #paradigm #beats #mckee #field #truby #snyder

---

## SUBTEXT

**Universal Definition** (McKee): The true meaning beneath the surface of dialogue - what characters really think, feel, or want but don't say directly, creating depth and realism through indirect expression.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story - most comprehensive), Seger (Making Great), Field (Screenplay), Apatow (Sick in Head)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Essential principle; dialogue's true meaning
- Seger: Creating layers in dialogue
- Field: Indirect expression technique
- Apatow: Balancing truth with subtext

**Tags**: #dialogue #dialogue_foundation #subtext #indirection #mckee #seger #field

---

## THEME

**Universal Definition**: The central idea, moral argument, or philosophical statement the story makes about human nature, society, or existence, typically expressed through character change and story events rather than stated directly.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story - as "controlling idea"), Truby (Anatomy - as "moral premise"), Field (Screenplay), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat - "Theme Stated"), Owen (Adaptation), Hutcheon (Theory of Adaptation)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: "Controlling idea" - story's ultimate meaning
- Truby: "Moral premise" - moral argument
- Field: Theme as underlying idea
- Snyder: "Theme Stated" at page 5
- Seger: Theme integration without preachiness
- Hutcheon: Theme preservation in adaptation

**Tags**: #theme #theme_theory #controlling_idea #moral_premise #meaning #mckee #truby #snyder

---

## THREE-ACT STRUCTURE

**Universal Definition**: The most common screenplay structure model dividing the story into Setup (Act One), Confrontation (Act Two), and Resolution (Act Three), with major plot points at act breaks.

**Source Coverage**: Field (Screenplay - definitive), McKee (Story), Seger (Making Great), Snyder (Save the Cat), Truby (Anatomy - rejects)

**Key Variations**:
- Field: Paradigm with specific page counts
- McKee: Flexible three-act approach
- Snyder: Three acts with 15 beats
- Seger: Standard with problem-solving
- Truby: Rejects as limiting

**Tags**: #structure #structure_foundation #three_act #paradigm #field #mckee #snyder

---

## TURNING POINT

**Universal Definition**: A moment in the story where the action changes direction, the protagonist makes a significant decision, or story values shift, propelling the narrative forward.

**Source Coverage**: McKee (Story), Field (Screenplay - as "plot point"), Seger (Making Great)

**Key Variations**:
- McKee: Moments of change in scenes
- Field: Plot points as major turning points
- Seger: Multiple turning points throughout

**Tags**: #structure #turning_point #plot_point #reversal #mckee #field #seger

---

## YES AND

**Universal Definition**: The fundamental improvisational principle of accepting what your scene partner offers ("Yes") and adding your own contribution to build on it ("And"), creating collaborative creation rather than blocking.

**Source Coverage**: Johnstone (Impro - philosophy), Close (Truth in Comedy), Besser (Manual), Leonard/Yorton (Yes, And - business applications)

**Key Variations**:
- Johnstone: Acceptance philosophy foundation
- Close: Building game through "Yes And"
- Besser: UCB technique application
- Leonard/Yorton: Business collaboration applications

**Tags**: #comedy_improv #improvisation #yes_and #acceptance #building #johnstone #close #besser #leonard_yorton #collaboration
